And it doesn't stop at candles. The spray before guests arrive, the plug-in in the bedroom, even the water diffuser on the nightstand that's supposed to be the healthy choice: every one of them leaves something behind in the air your kids and pets breathe.

Here are the five alerts most people miss. Save the last one for the end: it's the one that matters most.

Alert #1

The “Healthy” Diffuser On Your Nightstand May Be Growing Mold.

Water diffusers feel like the clean option. Just water and a few drops of oil, right?

That tank sits full of standing water for days, warm and in the dark. It's exactly where mold and bacteria grow. And every time the diffuser runs, whatever is growing in there goes into the mist you breathe while you sleep.

That's why the EPA's advice for humidifiers, which mist water the same way, is so strict: empty it every day, clean it every third day. Skip a week and you're misting whatever grew in there.

Be honest: when did you last scrub yours?

Alert #3

What's In That “Fresh” Scent Isn't On The Label.

Turn over an air freshener, a room spray or a candle. You'll usually find one word: “fragrance.” In the U.S., that single word can stand for dozens of ingredients that companies don't have to name.

When researchers tested 25 popular fragranced products, air fresheners included, they found 133 different chemicals released into the air. 24 of them are classified as toxic or hazardous under U.S. federal law, and only one of the 133 was listed on any label.

You can't avoid what nobody tells you is there.

And if grown adults react to it, think about the smallest lungs in the house.

Alert #4

The Sneezing And Headaches You Blame On The Season May Be Coming From Your Candle.

Itchy eyes. A stuffy nose that shows up every evening. A headache by 9 p.m. Most of us blame pollen, the weather or a long day.

But fragrance is one of the most common triggers. In a national U.S. survey, about one in three adults said fragranced products like air fresheners and scented candles gave them health problems, from headaches to asthma attacks.

Now think about who else is in that room.

Kids breathe in more air for their body size than adults do, so the same candle reaches them harder. And vets warn that smoke and aerosol sprays irritate pets' airways too, birds and cats most of all.

And the last alert is the one almost nobody hears about.

Alert #5

Every Night You Light A Candle, Your Lungs Breathe What It Leaves Behind.

This is the one that matters most.

Candles and incense don't only release scent. Every flame releases smoke and soot. The EPA lists burning candles and incense among the sources of fine particles indoors, particles small enough to get deep into your lungs, and some can even reach your bloodstream.

One candle won't hurt you. But it's never one candle. It's every evening, in a closed room, all winter, for years. Add the propellants and synthetic fragrance chemicals from sprays and plug-ins, and it adds up where you spend the most time: indoor air can hold 2 to 5 times more of some pollutants than the air outside, and Americans spend about 90% of their time indoors, according to the EPA.

And then there's the flame itself:
