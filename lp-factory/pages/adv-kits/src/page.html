1

Your lungs breathe everything that burns or sprays.

A candle or a stick of incense doesn't only release scent. Every flame makes smoke and soot: the black ring inside the jar, the grey smudge on the ceiling above the shelf. It doesn't stay there. It drifts through the room you're sitting in.

What the EPA saysThe U.S. Environmental Protection Agency lists burning candles and incense among the sources of fine particles indoors. Those particles are small enough to travel deep into the lungs.

Sprays and plug-ins add their own load: propellants and synthetic fragrance chemicals that hang in a closed room long after the scent fades.

One evening won't hurt anyone. Years of breathing it every night, in a closed room with the windows shut for winter, is a different story, especially for anyone with asthma or sensitive lungs. And then there's the flame itself: candles start thousands of home fires in the U.S. every year, according to the National Fire Protection Association.

Sprays and candles can set off allergies in you, your kids and your pets.

Itchy eyes, a stuffy nose, a headache that shows up every evening. Most of us blame the season. Fragrance is one of the most common triggers.

One in three adultsIn a national U.S. survey, about a third of adults said fragranced products such as air fresheners and scented candles gave them health problems, from headaches to asthma attacks.

Kids breathe in more air for their body size than adults do, so the same room reaches them more. Vets advise keeping smoke and aerosol sprays away from pets too, birds and cats most of all.

Water diffusers grow mold and bacteria, then mist it into the room.

Water diffusers sit full of water for days. A warm tank of standing water is where mold and bacteria grow, and whatever grows in the tank goes into the mist along with your oil.

The EPA's adviceFor humidifiers, which mist water the same way, the EPA recommends emptying the tank every day and cleaning it every third day, because dirty tanks can release mold and bacteria into the air.

Most of us rinse it when we remember. Few of us remember.

Leaking diffusers ruin your furniture, one ring at a time.

Water diffusers get bumped, overfilled and moved. The water ends up on the dresser: white rings, a cloudy stain in the finish, a warped spot under the base. Reed diffusers tip over, and the oil can strip the finish off wood. Plug-ins drip down the wall under the outlet.

The scent was supposed to make the room look and feel better.
