Renewal recovery model — is the front-end investment worth it?

The verdict, in plain terms

Yes — every subscriber you buy for $139 returns about $142 of renewal profit within 180 days (1.02× your money back).

The machine starts printing money when any one of these happens:

  • CAC drops below $142 per subscriber (currently $139 — needs 2% cheaper), or
  • retention beats 73% staying 180 days (you assumed 75%), or
  • subscribers keep renewing past 180 days — every extra month after that is $35/subscriber of nearly pure profit.

The first real renewal wave bills next week — those numbers will tell us if the 75% assumption holds. Until then this is a model, not a promise.

How to read this — you have invested $24,626 so far (everything spent minus everything earned). “If ads stop”, the 219 subscribers you already own keep renewing and pay back 121% of that within 180 days. “If ads continue” at today’s pace (3.4 new subscribers/day, $335/day front-end loss), the total hole grows for a while — but renewals stack until around day 14, when a normal day becomes profitable. Assumptions: 75% of subscribers renew on schedule through day 180 (the rest never renew), renewals at plan list price, ~$15 all-in fulfillment per single-scent renewal ($9 product + $6.00 shipping) plus card fees, nothing counted past each subscriber’s 180th day.

Methodology — Net sales are product revenue after discounts, before tax; taxes are excluded as pass-through. Refunds are booked on the day issued and do not credit COGS back. COGS uses Shopify line-item unit costs. Payment fees are the actual per-transaction fees from Shopify Payments. Shipping cost is a flat estimate per order — Shopify’s API does not expose purchased label costs; replace the estimate in report/config.json when the real average is known. Blended ROAS = net sales ÷ Meta spend (all revenue, not just attributed). Meta-reported ROAS uses Meta pixel attribution. Subscription MRR normalizes every Subi contract to a 30-day month.
