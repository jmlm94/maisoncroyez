## Key Question 1: Consumption reality — how refill consumption varies by intensity and hours per day, what brands state, what customers report, and what happens when stated cadence and real usage diverge

### Takeaway
Every major waterless/cold-air brand publishes a lifespan that is conditional on a medium setting and a limited daily schedule (typically 4-10 hours/day), and the resulting real-world range is roughly 2x to 4x wide (for example Pura 2-3 weeks on high vs 8-10 weeks on low; Aroma360 20-40 days on high vs 60-90 days on low). A flat "30 days" claim will therefore be wrong for most customers in one direction or the other, and the category's own review and complaint literature shows both failure modes: "cartridges didn't last as long as advertised" (Aera) and "refills add up" / vials accumulating (Pura).

**Cadence-mismatch consequences (cross-category)**
- "When you ship faster than consumption, you create inventory pile-up, guilt, skip behavior, and eventually cancellations." — [RetentionX: Subscription Pace — How 30-Day Cycles Kill Customer Retention](https://newsletter.retentionx.com/p/your-subscription-is-too-fast)
- "If someone says 'too much product,' the fix is to recommend a longer cadence or a smaller plan, not a discount." — [Appstle: Subscription Cadence Strategies / cadence mismatch](https://appstle.com/blog/shopify-subscription-cadence-mismatch/)
- Ordergroove "uses AI to anticipate when a subscriber is at risk of churn due to overstock and sends them an email offering them the option to skip their next order." — [Ordergroove: Subscription Retention & Churn Prevention](https://www.ordergroove.com/retain/)

### Inferences
- Implied consumption rates from the published claims: AromaTech AroMini 120 ml / (28 days x 8 h) = about 0.54 ml per hour on its default setting; Aroma360 120 ml / 300-600 h = 0.20-0.40 ml per hour across settings; Hotel Collection 120 ml / (30 days x 4-8 h) = 0.5-1.0 ml per hour. A reasonable planning band for a cold-air unit on medium is therefore roughly 0.3-0.6 ml per hour.
- Applied to Maison Croyez's 100 ml refill: at 0.5 ml/h the bottle is about 200 diffusion hours. That is about 33 days on the 6-hour timer, about 17 days on the 12-hour timer, about 67 days on the 3-hour timer, and roughly half those figures on the high intensity mode if high draws about 2x medium (the Aroma360 and Pura ranges imply a 2-3x spread between low and high). The "about 30 days of regular use" claim only holds for a fairly narrow band (about 6 h/day on medium).
- The planned 50 ml size is best positioned as the 3-hour-timer / low-intensity / small-room SKU and as a discovery or second-room format, not as a lower price rung; at published rates it lasts about 100 diffusion hours.
- Because a 12-hour-timer customer on high mode would run dry in roughly 8-10 days, a flat 30-day cadence will produce "ran out early" churn on that segment and "piling up" churn on the 1-3 hour segment. Cadence should be derived from timer plus mode at signup (see Key Question 2).

### Gaps
- Could not open Pura's duration help page to reconcile "120 hours" versus "350 hours / 15 days at medium"; the excerpt may conflate schedule days with active hours.
- No brand publishes an ml-per-hour table by intensity setting; the rates above are derived from stated bottle life and schedule, not from a manufacturer specification.
- No quantitative data found on churn rates attributable specifically to cadence mismatch in home fragrance; only qualitative evidence (complaints about cartridges not lasting; refills adding up).
- Could not access Reddit r/HomeFragrance or r/Pura threads; the customer-report evidence above comes from review sites and aggregators.

## Key Question 2: Cadence and size design — 30/45/60-day cadences, multi-bottle boxes, usage-based cadence recommendation at signup

### Takeaway
Category incumbents already offer a wider cadence menu than 30 days (AromaTech 1-4 months; Scentiment defaults to every 3 months; Aera lets subscribers change frequency freely), and the subscription-platform literature converges on the same design rule: let the customer's real consumption set the cadence, ideally via a quiz that does the math, and recommend a longer cadence or smaller plan rather than a discount when product piles up.

### Cited Findings
- AromaTech: "select your desired delivery frequency from one to four months before adding the oil, and you'll always get 20% off your refills, with flexible options to change delivery date, skip or cancel at any time." — [AromaTech: Subscription Scenting](https://aromatechscent.com/pages/subscription-scenting)
- Scentiment: subscribe-and-save "refills you automatically on a schedule you choose"; the default described is "Delivery occurs every 3 months, and you can change, skip, or cancel anytime"; subscribers get "10% off all fragrances"; "every third refill ships with a complimentary 20 ml bottle of a hotel-inspired scent"; "The price you pay on your first refill is the price you pay forever" — [Scentiment FAQs](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/faqs); [Scentiment Membership](https://www.scentiment.com/products/membership)
- Aera: subscribers "can change the frequency, swap, skip, add, pause or remove any time (with a 2-shipment minimum)"; 15% off first subscription order, 10% on future deliveries; 5% on Aera Mini fragrances — [Aera: Subscribe and Save](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/subscriptions); [Aera FAQ](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/faq)
- Pura: subscribers "can swap fragrances to get a different scent every month" and "adjust delivery times to get scents right when they want them"; alert sent "3 days before their subscription processes" — [Hello Subscription: 5 Reasons You Will Love Pura](https://hellosubscription.com/5-reasons-you-will-love-pura-premium-home-fragrances-that-elevate-your-space/). Pura's older auto-ship page is titled "Weekly Auto-Ship Subscription," indicating cadences shorter than monthly have been offered historically — [Pura (old site): Home Fragrance Weekly Auto-Ship Subscription](https://old.pura.com/pages/subscriptions)
- Quiz-driven cadence example: pet-care brand YuMOVE "uses a quiz to ask pet owners for their dog's weight to recommend a suitable number of supplement tablets for monthly subscription." — [Swanky: Subscription Retention Strategies](https://swankyagency.com/subscription-retention-strategies-reduce-churn-increase-ltv/)
- "The quiz does the cadence math the customer would otherwise skip. A quiz resolves both the recommendation and the commitment to recurring delivery in the same flow." — [RevenueHunt: Product quiz subscriptions](https://revenuehunt.com/product-quiz-subscriptions/)
- "Analyzing real consumption patterns by tracking skips, delays, and reorder gaps, and offering flexible delivery frequencies (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, quarterly) helps subscribers find a cadence that fits them." — [Blustream: Reduce Subscription Churn](https://blustream.ai/blog/reduce-subscription-churn-proven-retention-tips)
- Claim from a practitioner guide (unverified, single source): "Adjusting the subscription in real time through re-quizzing can turn a 3-month churn pattern into 18-month retention." — [Medium (Sunny Dodeja): Expert-Level Practical Guide to DTC Subscription Brands](https://medium.com/@gdoitwebpvtltd/expert-level-practical-guide-to-dtc-subscription-brands-069f4812d4e4)
- Flexibility effect on churn (aggregator statistic, original study not identified): "Platforms with skip, pause, swap, and gift options see churn drop by 32% compared to those stuck with just 'cancel or keep'." — [ChurnCost: E-commerce Subscription Churn Calculator + 2026 Benchmarks](https://churncost.com/ecommerce)
- Trade Coffee's quiz "matches customers to 500+ coffees from 55+ top roasters" and the company "is combating churn by turning its attention to recommendation tools"; CMO quote: "Most users don't want to try random new things, so we need to address this by building specific tools to minimize dissatisfaction." — [Modern Retail: How subscription coffee service Trade is planning for post-pandemic retention](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/we-dont-want-to-be-a-coffee-of-the-month-club-how-subscription-coffee-service-trade-is-planning-for-post-pandemic-retention/)
- Aggregator claim (original study not identified): "Brands offering online tasting quizzes and algorithm-based personalization achieved 34% higher retention compared to services with static offerings." — [Swell: 32 Coffee Subscription Trends](https://www.swell.is/content/coffee-subscription-trends)

### Inferences
- For Maison Croyez, the three device inputs (mode: low/medium/high; timer: 1/3/6/12 h; rooms) are sufficient to compute a recommended cadence without a long quiz. A three-question signup step ("Which timer will you mostly use? Which mode? How many rooms?") maps directly to a 30/45/60/90-day cadence or to 1 vs 2 bottles per box.
- Multi-bottle boxes on longer cadences (for example 2 x 100 ml every 60 days, or 3 x 100 ml every 90 days) cut shipments per year and fit the category's existing 1-4 month menus; Scentiment's every-third-refill gift shows how to reward tenure inside a quarterly cadence.
- The 50 ml SKU is the natural fix for the "too much product" segment; per Appstle's rule, offer the smaller plan or longer cadence instead of a retention discount.

### Gaps
- No home fragrance brand was found that explicitly asks hours/day and intensity at signup to set cadence; the quiz precedent comes from pet supplements and coffee.
- No controlled data found comparing churn for 30- vs 45- vs 60-day cadences within a single consumable brand.
- The "32%" and "34%" figures are aggregator statistics without a traceable primary study; use with caution.

## Key Question 3: Multi-room and household expansion as retention — do multi-device customers churn less, and how brands sell the second device

### Takeaway
No public data was found from Pura, Aera, or any home fragrance brand quantifying churn for one-device versus multi-device households. What is visible is that every incumbent packages multi-device bundles and full-home sets, Pura's app supports adding a "second (or more)" diffuser, and reviewers routinely describe owning three devices across rooms and car, which is where the "$50-80/month" refill bills come from.

### Cited Findings
- Pura sells a "Full Home Bundle," a "Home Mini Bundle," and publishes a help article "How to: Add a Second (or more) Diffuser" — [Pura Full Home Bundle](https://pura.com/products/pura-full-home-bundle); [Pura Home Mini Bundle](https://pura.com/products/pura-mini-bundle); [Pura Support: Add a Second (or more) Diffuser](https://help.pura.com/en/features/second-wall-diffuser)
- Pura's "Free Set" terms allow the subscription commitment to be satisfied by stacking more subscriptions faster: "2 new home fragrance subscriptions... in 1 year... 3 new home fragrance subscriptions in 8 months, 4 new subscriptions in 6 months, 6 new subscriptions in 4 months, or 8 new subscriptions in 3 months." — [Pura: Discounted Diffuser Terms and Conditions](https://pura.com/pages/discounted-diffuser-terms-and-conditions)
- A reviewer describes "a Pura 3 in their bedroom, a Pura 4 in their living room, and a Pura Car in their car" — [Thingtesting: Pura reviews](https://thingtesting.com/brands/pura/reviews)
- Multi-device cost reality: "If you're running 3-4 diffusers, you can expect $50-80/month in vials" — [WeTried.it: Is Pura Worth It?](https://wetried.it/is-pura-worth-it/)
- Aera's device line (Aera, Aera Mini, Aera Go) uses non-interchangeable capsules: "capsules for each diffuser are different... each requires its own compatible capsule size" — [Aera FAQ](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/faq)
- Scentiment extends the warranty on "every Scentiment diffuser they own" for active subscribers — [Scentiment FAQs](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/faqs)
- Pura's third-party refill partnerships (Nest New York, Capri Blue, LAFCO, Anthropologie) are credited by a business-model analysis with "increasing refill attachment rates and average revenue per user" — [Streamlinefeed: Pura smart diffuser home fragrance economics](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/pura-smart-diffuser-home-fragrance-economics) (secondary source; no numbers given)

### Inferences
- Maison Croyez already has a structural advantage over Aera: one 100 ml refill fits every device, so a second device raises refill consumption without fragmenting SKUs.
- The existing 1/3/5-diffuser kits are the acquisition-side version of multi-room; the retention-side version is selling device number two to an active subscriber without discounting hardware. The Pura and Scentiment precedents suggest two non-discount levers: (a) letting a second device count toward, or extend, subscriber-only benefits (warranty, points, gift refills); (b) framing the second device as the way to get a second scent running simultaneously (bedroom vs living room), which Pura's two-bay design does in a single unit.
- Because Maison Croyez never discounts the device, an "existing subscriber adds a device" offer should be structured as added fragrance value (for example a free 50 ml with the second device, or the subscriber's next box upgraded to two bottles) rather than a hardware price cut.

### Gaps
- No published churn or LTV comparison for one- versus multi-device households from Pura, Aera, Aroma360, or Sonos-style analogs was found; search budget ended before a Sonos multi-product attach source could be retrieved.
- Could not retrieve Pura's in-app second-device flow or any "add a device" offer targeted to existing subscribers.

## Key Question 4: Discovery and rotation formats — sample sets, discovery vials, scent-of-the-month, seasonal limited scents, build-a-box, and their effect on retention and AOV

### Takeaway
Fragrance and coffee subscriptions that report low churn (Scentbird at about 9% versus a 12% industry figure; Atlas Coffee Club's country-of-the-month) attribute it to rotation and queue mechanics that give the subscriber a reason to stay curious, while Trade's data shows the opposite risk: most users "don't want to try random new things," so rotation must be opt-in and guided. Discovery formats in home fragrance are already common (Scentiment 3-scent kits, Aera test plugs, Pura Scent Club), and Snif's 7-day try-before-you-buy hold is the most aggressive de-risking mechanic in the category.

### Cited Findings

**Scentbird (perfume subscription, rotation model)**
- 700,000 subscribers in 2024, up from 450,000 in 2022 — [WWD: Scentbird founder interview, March 2024](https://wwd.com/beauty-industry-news/beauty-features/perfume-fragrance-scentbird-subscription-anniversary-growth-founder-interview-1236251502/)
- Analyst summary: FY2025 "720 SKUs and reported a churn rate near 9%, below industry avg 12%"; "$16.95 monthly plan is the backbone... driving a 92% retention among 'Fragrance Enthusiasts'"; "Scentbird Rewards revamp (2024)... gamification cut churn ~8% YoY"; "about 62% of users manage fragrance queues via the app, boosting monthly selection rates by 28% and reducing churn by 12% year-over-year (2025 data)" — [CanvasBusinessModel: Scentbird Marketing Mix](https://canvasbusinessmodel.com/products/scentbird-marketing-mix) and [Scentbird SWOT](https://canvasbusinessmodel.com/products/scentbird-swot-analysis) (secondary analyst source; figures not verified against Scentbird)
- Scentbird's CTO: the company "initially had separate teams with different KPIs for acquisition and retention, which led to low-quality subscribers and higher churn. Now, their entire strategy is built around one core metric — lifetime value (LTV)." — [The Subscription Doc, Dose #139: Scaling Subscription Success (Andrei Rebrov)](https://newsletter.thesubscriptiondoc.com/p/dose-139)

**Snif (DTC fragrance, try-before-you-buy)**
- "Once your Snif package arrives, you have 7 days to try out the sample fragrance inside... During the trial period, the company places a temporary 'hold' on your card." Bundle: "three full-size, 30-ml fragrances and three petite 2-millimeter [sic] samples." — [Snif: How It Works](https://snif.co/pages/how-it-works); [The Fascination: Snif Original Bundle Review](https://thefascination.com/lander/stories/snif-review/)
- Trade press framing: "Curiosity Over Loyalty" — Snif's DTC audience "thrives on blind-buy excitement and experimental scents" — [Perfumer & Flavorist: Curiosity Over Loyalty: Snif](https://www.perfumerflavorist.com/fragrance/fine-fragrance/article/22963780/curiosity-over-loyalty-snif-and-the-rise-of-experimental-fragrance-for-a-new-generation)

**Coffee (Trade, Atlas)**
- Trade: "Most users don't want to try random new things"; retention effort focused on recommendation tools and small-roaster partnerships — [Modern Retail: Trade retention](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/we-dont-want-to-be-a-coffee-of-the-month-club-how-subscription-coffee-service-trade-is-planning-for-post-pandemic-retention/)
- Atlas Coffee Club: "Featuring a new country's coffee each month gave subscribers a reason to stick around, recognizing that a bored customer is one step away from churning"; uses "a carefully timed email sequence including a preview email, brew guide, and post-tasting survey to turn a monthly coffee subscription into a habit"; co-founder: "it's 10 times more expensive to get a new customer than keep an existing one" — [Email Marketers Newsletter: How Atlas Coffee Club brewed up a $22m subscription](https://emailmarketersnewsletter.com/p/atlas-coffee-club-brewed-22m-subscription-klaviyo-attribution-tips); [Retention Marketing Podcast: How Atlas Coffee Club Uses Email](https://music.amazon.com/podcasts/b3687e98-38ef-45ee-9633-a7ec1011f19d/episodes/6b4484de-a7a7-431d-ba4c-0ae63fa64f1e/the-retention-marketing-podcast-with-fexingo-email-sms-and-loyalty-for-repeat-customers-how-atlas-coffee-club-uses-email-to-drive-subscription-retention)

**Home fragrance discovery formats**
- Scentiment sells a "Diffuser Air 2 Discovery Kit | 3 Luxury Hotel Scents" — [Scentiment Discovery Kit](https://www.scentiment.com/products/scent-diffuser-discovery-kit-v2)
- Aera sells "Extra Capsule Plugs for testing" as an accessory, so a capsule can be opened, sampled, and resealed — [Aera: Extra Capsule Plugs](https://www.aeraforhome.com/products/extra-capsule-plugs-for-testing)
- Pura offers a "Scent Club" subscription product — [Pura: Scent Club](https://pura.com/products/scent-club-subscription) (page not retrievable; format and price unconfirmed)
- Pura's refill catalog is built on licensed third-party scent brands (Capri Blue, LAFCO, Anthropologie, Nest), sold as 2-packs — [Amazon: Capri Blue Pura refills 2-pack](https://www.amazon.com/Capri-Blue-Smart-Diffuser-Refills/dp/B0BWCFYCG3); [LAFCO: Pura refills](https://www.lafco.com/home-fragrance/pura-smart-fragrance-diffuser)
- Scentiment's tenure gift: "every third refill ships with a complimentary 20 ml bottle of a hotel-inspired scent" — [Scentiment FAQs](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/faqs)

### Inferences
- With seven scents, Maison Croyez cannot run a true "new scent every month" program for long; the workable analogs are (a) a 7 x small-vial discovery set included with the device (which also solves the "which scent do I subscribe to" decision), (b) a seasonal or limited eighth scent once or twice a year, and (c) the Scentiment-style tenure gift (a 50 ml of a different scent on every third box), which doubles as scent discovery and a swap prompt.
- Trade's finding argues for a default of "same scent, swap anytime" with rotation as an opt-in toggle, not a forced surprise box.
- Snif's 7-day hold is a device-level analog: a 30-day "love it or send it back" on the diffuser is the closest equivalent for an $80-90 device and is already implicit in Pura's 30-day cancel-and-return clause.

### Gaps
- No data on AOV lift from build-a-box or discovery kits in home fragrance specifically.
- Scentbird figures come from a business-model analysis site, not Scentbird disclosures; the WWD and Subscription Doc pages could not be opened to verify.
- Pura Scent Club structure (price, cadence, surprise vs chosen) could not be retrieved.

## Key Question 5: Free-first-refill and device-led acquisition — converting device purchase into active subscription, attach-rate benchmarks, and what Pura, Aera, Aroma360 and Hotel Collection do

### Takeaway
The category has largely inverted the razor-and-blades offer: rather than "buy the device, get free fragrance," Pura, Aroma360 and Hotel Collection now give the device away against a fragrance commitment (Pura: free Pura 4 with 2 fragrances/month for 6 months, or free device plus 2 scents with a 12-month commitment; Aroma360/Hotel Collection: free diffuser with 3-month minimum and a $50 or $99 early-termination fee). Aera and AromaTech keep the softer model (device paid, first-order discount, first capsule free promotions). No public attach-rate benchmark for device-to-subscription conversion in this category was found.

### Cited Findings

**Pura**
- Offer as of September 2025: "subscribe to 2 fragrances for 6 months and get a FREE next-gen Pura 4 device"; alternative: "free diffuser and 2 free scents when you subscribe to 2 fragrances monthly for a year"; non-commitment option "15% off your first order using coupon code HELLOSUB" — [Hello Subscription: Pura Coupon (Sept 2025)](https://hellosubscription.com/2025/09/pura-holiday-coupon/); [Pura: Free Pura Set](https://pura.com/products/free-pura-set); [Pura: Free Diffuser](https://pura.com/pages/free-diffuser)
- Terms: "At checkout, you will be charged a monthly subscription amount for the fragrance(s)"; "you can cancel your subscription within 30 days and return your diffuser without penalty, otherwise you will be charged the amount initially discounted at its listed offer price for any cancellation prior to fulfilling the terms of the commitment"; must cancel "five (5) days before it renews" — [Pura: Discounted Diffuser Terms and Conditions](https://pura.com/pages/discounted-diffuser-terms-and-conditions)
- Pura also runs a "first month free" product page — [Pura: First Month Free](https://pura.com/products/first-month-free) (structure not retrievable)

**Consequences of the committed-device model**
- Aroma360 complaint pattern: customers "who attempted to cancel due to equipment failures were told they could cancel but would still be responsible for early termination fees"; "patterns of difficulty cancelling subscriptions, problems with returns, aggressive sales tactics, confusing financing agreements" — [BBB: Aroma360 complaints](https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/miami/profile/essential-oils/aroma360-llc-0633-90405374/complaints); [Trustpilot: Aroma360](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/aroma360.com); [PissedConsumer: Aroma360](https://aroma360.pissedconsumer.com/review.html)

### Inferences
- Maison Croyez's current offer (full-price device plus 30 days of fragrance free) is closest to Aera's model and is the premium-consistent choice; the Pura/Aroma360 path buys attach rate with hardware subsidy and commitment fees, and the Aroma360 and Hotel Collection complaint records show the reputational cost.
- The leak point in the Maison Croyez structure is the moment the free 100 ml runs out with no subscription in place. Two mechanics from the category close it without discounting the device: (a) require the subscription to be created at device checkout, with the first shipment free and the first paid shipment scheduled for day 30 (Pura's "first month free" and Aera's FREEFRAGRANCE both attach the free product to a subscription, not to the device); (b) make the free refill contingent on subscription, so a non-subscriber pays for fragrance from day one.
- Because the free refill's real life will be 10-60 days depending on timer and mode, the first paid shipment date should be set from the cadence quiz, not fixed at day 30.

### Gaps

- The SBI Growth case study's "up to 50% more revenue" claim could not be read in context.

## Key Question 6: Prepaid and gift structures — 3/6/12-month prepaid, gifting, and their effect on churn and LTV

### Takeaway
Cross-category subscription data consistently shows prepaid/annual plans cut cancellation materially (a widely cited "40% less likely to cancel" figure; Ordergroove reports a 2.2x year-over-year prepaid revenue lift across its merchant base), and Pura's own commitment tiers (6- and 12-month) are a disguised prepaid model. No home-fragrance-specific prepaid or gift retention data was found.

### Cited Findings
- "Ordergroove reported a 2.2x year-over-year prepaid revenue lift and 67% year-over-year active subscription growth across their merchant base." — [Digital Applied: Subscription Commerce 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/subscription-commerce-2026-recurring-revenue-guide) (secondary; original Ordergroove report not retrieved)
- "Annual plans reduce churn by 40% versus monthly billing, with annual subscribers 40% less likely to cancel than monthly subscribers"; "If you do not offer or actively migrate to a multi-month prepay plan, you are leaving 15-30 points of annual retention on the table." — [Eightx: Average ecommerce subscription churn by billing period, 2026 benchmark](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-subscription-churn-by-billing-period-2026) (page not retrievable; figures from indexed excerpt)
- Baseline: "At 6% monthly churn — the industry average for physical subscription boxes — you lose nearly half your subscriber base every year." — [ChurnCost: E-commerce benchmarks](https://churncost.com/ecommerce)
- Recharge merchant-base retention curve: "45% retention at 6 months and 33% retention at 12 months on average"; 2025 report: "gap between top-quartile and bottom-quartile subscription brands in net revenue retention has widened to 34 percentage points" — [Swell: 40 Subscription Commerce Statistics for 2025](https://www.swell.is/content/subscription-commerce-statistics) (citing Recharge; page not retrievable)
- "Replenishment subscriptions (consumables, pet food, household goods) typically run 5-8% monthly churn"; "Replenishment subscriptions retain roughly 45% of subscribers at 12 months while meal kits drop under 15%" — [Eightx: Average Subscription Churn Rate by Category 2026](https://eightx.co/blog/average-subscription-churn-rate-by-category); [SubJolt: DTC Subscription Retention Playbook](https://www.subjolt.com/guides/dtc-subscription-retention/)
- Ordergroove's program types "enable merchants to offer bundles, prepaid plans, gifts, digital products, committed terms, rotating selections, and free trials" — [Ordergroove Knowledge Center: Feature Descriptions](https://help.ordergroove.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052367594-Ordergroove-Feature-Descriptions)
- Pura's commitment structures function as prepaid-equivalents: 6-month (2 fragrances/month, free Pura 4) and 12-month (2 fragrances/month, free device plus 2 scents), with the device discount clawed back on early cancellation — [Hello Subscription: Pura Coupon (Sept 2025)](https://hellosubscription.com/2025/09/pura-holiday-coupon/); [Pura: Discounted Diffuser Terms](https://pura.com/pages/discounted-diffuser-terms-and-conditions)
- Aera's softer commitment device is a "2-shipment minimum" on subscriptions — [Aera FAQ](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/faq)

### Inferences
- A premium brand that refuses hardware discounts can still use prepaid: a 6- or 12-month fragrance prepay (6 x 100 ml or 12 x 100 ml, shipped on the customer's cadence) at the same 25% subscriber price, with the reward delivered as fragrance (for example the seventh bottle free, or a 50 ml discovery of each of the other scents) rather than as a deeper discount. This mirrors Pura's economics without Pura's clawback fees.
- Gift subscriptions in this category are naturally device-led (a gift kit of diffuser plus 3 months of fragrance); the retention question is whether the recipient converts to a paying subscriber at month four. No data was found; this should be instrumented as its own cohort.

### Gaps
- No home fragrance brand publishes prepaid or gift-subscription retention data.
- The Eightx and Swell pages could not be opened to check methodology behind the "40%" and "45%/33%" figures; they are aggregator statistics.
- No data on gift-to-paid conversion rates for consumable subscriptions (Scentbird gift plans, Trade gift plans) was retrieved before the search budget ended.

## Key Question 7: Packaging and unboxing as retention — refill packaging that supports the ritual, and evidence it affects repeat rate

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly vendor-published. The strongest concrete mechanics found are Atlas Coffee Club's per-shipment content sequence (preview, guide, post-tasting survey) that is designed to make each delivery a ritual, Diptyque's refill packaging that keeps the luxury vessel and makes the refill the "better value" object, and QR-on-insert tracking that lets a brand measure whether second purchases follow a scan. No study specific to home fragrance refills was found.

### Cited Findings
- Atlas Coffee Club pairs every shipment with "a preview email, brew guide, and post-tasting survey to turn a monthly coffee subscription into a habit and keep each shipment feel personal" — [Email Marketers Newsletter: Atlas Coffee Club](https://emailmarketersnewsletter.com/p/atlas-coffee-club-brewed-22m-subscription-klaviyo-attribution-tips)
- Diptyque's 2026 classic-candle relaunch makes the vessel refillable for the first time in 63 years: 190 g candle $90, 300 g candle $130, refill $70; refills "sold in a casing made of 'paperfoam,' a bio-sourced, recyclable material"; reusing the vessel "can reduce the candle's carbon footprint by around 22%"; "Diptyque's refills already represent a big share of the brand's fragrance business" — [WWD: Diptyque's Iconic Candle Gets First Soft Lift](https://wwd.com/beauty-industry-news/beauty-features/exclusive-diptyques-iconic-candle-first-soft-lift-1238892302/); [Cosmetics Business: Diptyque Classic Candle becomes refillable](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/diptyque-classic-candle-becomes-refillable-for-first-time); [Homes and Gardens: Diptyque relaunch](https://www.homesandgardens.com/solved/diptyque-relaunches-iconic-candles-more-sustainable)
- QR-insert evidence (vendor-published): "traceability scans convert into repeat purchase at roughly double the rate of non-scanners in food and beverage categories"; brands can "track scan rate per thousand packs and repeat purchases in pilot stores versus matched controls, using QR code packaging as a clean predictor of second purchase" — [CustomerThink: 7 Ways QR Codes on Packaging are Winning Trust and Repeat Purchases](https://customerthink.com/7-ways-qr-codes-on-packaging-are-winning-trust-and-repeat-purchases/); [Ecommerce Fastlane: QR codes on packaging and inserts](https://ecommercefastlane.com/shopify-qr-codes-packaging-flyers-product-inserts-sales/)
- "A QR code inside the box may be better for product education, review requests, or repeat purchase offers" — [Quick QR Tools: QR Codes for Packaging Inserts](https://quickqrtools.com/qr-codes-for-packaging-inserts/)
- Scentbird's app-managed queue (62% of users) is linked in analyst material to a 28% lift in monthly selection and 12% YoY churn reduction — [CanvasBusinessModel: Scentbird SWOT](https://canvasbusinessmodel.com/products/scentbird-swot-analysis) (secondary; not verified)

### Inferences
- The refill box is Maison Croyez's only recurring physical touchpoint, so the Atlas pattern applies directly: a card per scent with a timer-and-mode suggestion ("3 hours, medium, for bedtime"), an intention prompt, and a QR that opens the swap/cadence page. The QR gives the brand a measurable pre-churn signal (scan rate by cohort) that most home fragrance brands lack because they have no app.
- Diptyque's refill move is the premium precedent for making the refill feel like the luxury object, not the cheaper substitute; packaging the 100 ml as the "hero" with a keepsake outer box for the diffuser is the same logic.

### Gaps
- No controlled study linking packaging inserts or intention cards to repeat rate in fragrance or any consumable was found; QR figures are from QR vendors.
- Could not open the Diptyque or Cosmetics Business pages to confirm the refill launch date and market availability.

## Key Question 8: Product quality issues that drive churn — device failures, leaks, weak throw, clogs — and warranty/replacement programs as retention tools

### Takeaway
The category's public complaint record is dominated by device failure inside the first months (Aroma360: units failing "within approximately one week" and "after about 3 months"), leaking vials that damage furniture (Pura), and a major safety recall (over 851,400 Pura 4 units, 2025). The retention response the incumbents converge on is a warranty that is longer for subscribers: Pura extends a 1-year warranty to lifetime for active subscribers, Aera extends 1 year to 3 years for app registration, and Scentiment extends its 1-year warranty across every device a subscriber owns.

### Cited Findings

**Failure modes**
- Pura leaking guidance: "If your vial is leaking, Pura suggests placing the leaking vial in a plastic or glass bowl. The concentrated oils can dissolve and remove varnish and other finishes on certain materials, such as wooden furniture." — [Pura Support: Fragrance is Leaking](https://help.pura.com/en/troubleshooting/Leaking-Fragrance-in-Diffuser)
- Pura recall: "Over 851,400 Pura 4 Smart Home Fragrance Diffusers have been recalled, affecting devices sold between August 2023 and May 2025 due to a choking hazard from detachable magnets"; remedy is a free replacement front cover via 855-394-5292 or replacement@pura.com — [Yahoo News: Nearly One Million Pura Fragrance Diffusers Recalled](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/nearly-one-million-pura-fragrance-130025040.html); [Yahoo News: Diffusers Recalled from Target, Amazon](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/diffusers-recalled-target-amazon-due-164329631.html)

- Scentiment's Trustpilot presence exists but content could not be retrieved — [Trustpilot NL: Scentiment](https://nl.trustpilot.com/review/scentiment.com)

**Warranty as a subscriber benefit**
- Pura: "All Pura diffusers come with a one-year warranty from the original purchase date... If you maintain an active Pura subscription your warranty will be extended through the lifespan of your subscription"; marketed as "a lifetime manufacturing warranty on their diffusers" — [Pura Support: Diffuser Warranty Policy](https://help.pura.com/en/orders_shipping/Diffuser-Warranty-Policy); [Hello Subscription: 5 Reasons You Will Love Pura](https://hellosubscription.com/5-reasons-you-will-love-pura-premium-home-fragrances-that-elevate-your-space/)

### Inferences
- For a device that is never discounted, a subscriber-only extended warranty is the cleanest non-price benefit: it costs nothing until a failure, it converts a churn event (broken device) into a retention event (free replacement), and it gives the customer a concrete reason not to pause. (Inference; no source found on this specific issue.)

## Key Question 9: Pricing architecture — how a 25% subscription discount compares to category norms, and whether membership or points beats a flat discount for premium positioning

### Takeaway
A 25% subscribe-and-save discount is at the top of the home fragrance range (Pura 20%, AromaTech 20%, Aera 10-15%, Scentiment 10%); luxury refill programs cluster around a 20-22% refill saving without any subscription (Le Labo about 20%, Diptyque refill $70 versus $90). Pura layers points (double on subscription orders), free shipping and a lifetime warranty on top of its 20%; Scentiment adds price-lock and a tenure gift. The retention literature places paid membership in the low-churn quadrant because "the fee itself is a commitment device," but no head-to-head test against a flat discount in consumables was found.

### Cited Findings

**Category subscription discounts**
- Pura: 20% off subscribed fragrance, double rewards points on subscription purchases, free standard shipping, lifetime warranty, cancel anytime — [Hello Subscription: 5 Reasons You Will Love Pura](https://hellosubscription.com/5-reasons-you-will-love-pura-premium-home-fragrances-that-elevate-your-space/); [Pura: Subscribe and Save](https://pura.com/pages/subscribe-and-save)
- Aera: 15% off first subscription order, 10% thereafter; 5% on Aera Mini fragrances; 2-shipment minimum — [Aera: Subscribe and Save](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/subscriptions); [Aera FAQ](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/faq)
- AromaTech: 20% off refills on 1-4 month cadence — [AromaTech: Subscription Scenting](https://aromatechscent.com/pages/subscription-scenting)
- Scentiment: 10% off, first-refill price locked "forever," extended warranty, free 20 ml every third refill — [Scentiment FAQs](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/faqs); [Scentiment Membership](https://www.scentiment.com/products/membership)
- Aroma360 and Hotel Collection: device-subsidy model with 3-month commitment and $50/$99 early-termination fees rather than a headline percentage — [Aroma360: Free Diffuser Promo Policy](https://aroma360.com/pages/free-diffuser-promo-policy); [Hotel Collection: Free Diffuser Promo Policy](https://www.hotelcollection.com/pages/free-diffuser-promo-policy)

**Premium refill programs without subscription discounting**
- Le Labo: refills for 50 ml, 100 ml and 500 ml eau de parfum in-store and online at "approximately 20% off retail price" — [Le Labo: Refill Program](https://www.lelabofragrances.com/le-labo-refill-program.html); [Le Labo: Refill Program FAQ](https://www.lelabofragrances.com/faq/refill-program.html)
- Diptyque: classic candle $90, refill $70 (about 22% saving) — [WWD: Diptyque's Iconic Candle Gets First Soft Lift](https://wwd.com/beauty-industry-news/beauty-features/exclusive-diptyques-iconic-candle-first-soft-lift-1238892302/)
- Aesop: no established consumer refill program; "teams researching refill and circular solutions" — [Aesop US Assistance: Does Aesop offer product refills?](https://us.assistance.aesop.com/hc/en-us/articles/7406481519503-Does-Aesop-offer-product-refills); Aesop runs a loyalty page — [Aesop: Loyalty](https://www.aesop.com/loyalty/) (content not retrievable)

**Membership versus discount**
- "VIP membership and software subscriptions sit in the ideal quadrant for retention with high LTV potential and low churn... In contrast, curated and mystery boxes, content, and subscribe and save sit in the hardest quadrant because retention depends on continuously delivering perceived value rather than structural lock-in." — [SubJolt: DTC Subscription Retention Playbook by Model](https://www.subjolt.com/guides/dtc-subscription-retention/)
- "A flat annual fee buys free shipping, member pricing, early access, or exclusive products, and the fee itself is a commitment device." — [Paysight: Types of Subscriptions in Ecommerce](https://www.paysight.io/blogs/types-of-subscriptions-in-ecommerce-a-practical-guide-for-operators)
- "Adore Me built a $300M revenue lingerie brand entirely on paid membership, where members paid monthly and received store credit plus exclusive benefits"; aggregator claim that "brands using the membership model have achieved 115% higher LTV compared to traditional retail" — [Subscribfy: What is a DTC Subscription Brand, 2026 Guide](https://subscribfy.ai/blogs/what-is-a-dtc-subscription-brand-the-complete-2026-guide) (secondary; original source of the 115% figure not identified)
- Scentbird's 2024 rewards revamp is credited with cutting churn about 8% YoY — [CanvasBusinessModel: Scentbird SWOT](https://canvasbusinessmodel.com/products/scentbird-swot-analysis) (secondary)

### Inferences
- At 25%, Maison Croyez is discounting more deeply than any premium peer; at $40-50 list that is a $10-12.50 per-bottle giveaway. The peer set suggests two premium-consistent alternatives: (a) hold at 20% (Pura/AromaTech/Le Labo parity) and reinvest the 5 points in non-price benefits (subscriber warranty, tenure gift, price lock), or (b) keep 25% but present it as the member price behind a light annual membership, so the headline discount never appears on the product page.
- A points model only helps if there is something to redeem; with seven scents and one device, the redeemable catalog is thin. The 50 ml bottle and the second device are the obvious redemption items, which is a further argument for launching the 50 ml.
- The Diptyque and Le Labo refill economics (about 20% saving on a full-price vessel or bottle) show that luxury customers accept a modest refill saving as "the refill is the smart way to buy," which is a stronger premium frame than "subscribe and save 25%."

### Gaps
- No A/B or cohort data comparing a flat subscribe-and-save discount to a paid membership or points program in home fragrance or fine fragrance.
- Pura's rewards program mechanics (points per dollar, redemption catalog) and Aesop's loyalty terms could not be retrieved.
- Hotel Collection's headline subscribe-and-save percentage (outside the free-diffuser bundles) was not confirmed.
