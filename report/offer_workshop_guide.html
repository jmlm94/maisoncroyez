The one-paragraph version. Since July 1 you have spent $42,545 on Meta to bring in 350 new customers (269 of them subscribers) and $36,839 of Shopify sales. Front-end contribution before ads is $9,305, so the business is −$33,240 behind after ads, and renewals are what pay that back. The two best weeks (Jul 27 and Aug 17) bought subscribers at $87 each with a $39.95 entry price and a visibly free diffuser. Every move that raised the entry price, stretched billing to 45 days or removed the subscription made the week worse. Of 226 subscribers started, 155 are paying on schedule, 41 are stuck in failed payment, and only 5 have truly cancelled.

6. What the data says for the offer meeting

What worked

  • A $39.95 entry price with a visibly free diffuser. Both $87-per-subscriber weeks had exactly this. Nothing else you tried got below $100.
  • Tiers on the build-your-kit page. Letting people choose 1, 2 or 3 scents doubled the scents per subscriber at the same acquisition cost (Aug 17 vs Jul 27).
  • 30-day billing. The 30-day cohorts renew better than the 45-day ones, and the 45-day switch in early August coincided with a 65% jump in subscriber cost.
  • The E copy on video. "17 free diffusers left" plus the guarantee produced the best click-to-order ever (3.05%).
  • CBO over ABO. Every ABO test (Aug 26, Sep 21) lost to the CBO running the same creative.

What did not

  • Raising the entry price ($49.95 in August, $89.95 kits in September). Front margin per order went up, but subscriber cost went up faster.
  • Making the subscription optional. Sub share fell from 93% to 22–52% and the business is built on renewals, not first orders.
  • 45-day cadence. Fewer renewals per year and worse first-renewal rates.
  • Interest and lookalike ABO campaigns at $200+ CPM.

The economics any offer has to clear

  • At today's costs a $39.95 1+1 order loses about $4 at the front end; at $22.50/$4.50 it makes $3. The 2- and 3-scent tiers are positive under both.
  • A paying subscriber on one $39.95 scent contributes about $22 per renewal ($26 at target costs). Paying back a $158 subscriber takes 7 renewals today and 6 at target costs, so first-renewal rate and payment recovery matter more than the last $5 of price.
  • Payment recovery is the cheapest lever: 41 subscribers and $2,001/month are stuck on failed cards with a 21% recovery rate. Set the Subi fallback to Pause and text the update-card link.
  • Renewals are now 44% of September sales. Every month the base renews, the offer has to work less hard.
Suggested framing for the meeting. Keep the rule "you pay for the scents, the diffusers are free" and the 1/2/3 tiers. Decide whether the entry point should return to $39.95 (proven $87 subscribers, thin or negative first-order margin, needs the $22.50/$4.50 costs to break even) or stay at $49.95 (better margin, unproven at scale, one week of mixed data). Whatever the price, keep the subscription required and the plan at 30 days, put the money into the CBO with E-copy video, and fix Subi recovery before scaling spend.
