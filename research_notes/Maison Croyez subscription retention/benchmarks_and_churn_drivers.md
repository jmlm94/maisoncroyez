# Churn and Retention Benchmarks for Monthly DTC Replenishment Subscriptions (US, 2024-2026)

**Method note for the report writer.** Direct page fetches to getrecharge.com, recurly.com, ordergroove.com, loopwork.co, eightx.co, mckinsey.com, subjolt.com and darkroomagency.com were blocked by the network egress proxy in this session. Every figure below was captured from web-search result summaries of those pages, not from the full documents. Figures are therefore reliable as to headline numbers but exact definitions (e.g., how "churn" or "active churn" is computed) could not be verified. Vendor-reported = platform's own customer data (Recharge, Ordergroove, Loop, Recurly, Churn Buster, Stay AI). Aggregator = third-party blog restating vendor numbers (Eightx, Finsi, SubJolt, Everboost, Swell, Darkroom, shno.co). Where a figure could not be traced to a primary source it is flagged.

## Key Question 1: Typical monthly and annual churn for DTC replenishment subscriptions (overall and beauty/home/fragrance)

### Takeaway
Consumer physical-goods subscriptions run roughly 5-8% monthly churn (Recurly's consumer-goods benchmark is 6.5%; Recharge health & wellness ran ~4.2% in 2023), which compounds to only ~33% of a starting cohort still active at 12 months in Recharge's merchant-wide data (45% at 6 months). Beauty & Personal Care is repeatedly reported by Recharge as the lowest-churn vertical, with a 97.5% first-to-second-order conversion; there is no published home-fragrance-specific churn benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Across more than 15,000 subscription merchants, Recharge State of Subscription Commerce data shows 45% of subscribers still active at six months and 33% at twelve months (vendor data, restated by an aggregator; year of the underlying cohort not stated in the summary) — [Darkroom, citing Recharge](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/subscription-churn); also [Swell](https://www.swell.is/content/subscription-commerce-statistics)
- Recharge "2023 in Review: The Year of the Routine" (published Feb 2024, analysis of 15,000+ merchants): smaller merchants "experienced precipitous drops in retention, falling to a shaky 22% at the 12-month mark"; mid-sized brands (1,000-49,999 subscribers) saw reduced churn and increases in MRR, LTV and AOV; Beauty & Personal Care merchants posted an 82% gain in MRR in 2023 and were "2023's biggest winner, making the biggest gains in every KPI tracked" (vendor) — [PR Newswire / Recharge](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/recharge-releases-2023-in-review-the-year-of-the-routine-insights-report-302054874.html); [Recharge resource page](https://getrecharge.com/resources/2023-in-review/)
- Recharge data put health & wellness churn near 4.2% monthly in 2023, split about 3.0% voluntary + 1.2% involuntary (vendor data, restated by aggregator) — [Eightx, citing Recharge](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)
- Recharge Subscription Trend Report 2026 (20,000 brands): "Beauty & Personal Care merchants saw the lowest overall customer churn among verticals"; beauty second-order conversion is 97.5% vs health & wellness 92.9% and pet 96.2%; subscription checkouts vs one-time purchases up 16% year over year (vendor) — [Recharge Subscription Trend Report 2026](https://getrecharge.com/reports/subscription-trend-report-2026/); [Eightx summary](https://eightx.co/blog/average-subscription-churn-rate-by-category)
- Recharge per-vertical "active churn" (share of billing cycles ending in cancellation, per the summary): supplements 17.3% of cycles, coffee 20.7%, beauty 20.7%; pet lowest; supplements highest skip rate at 6.9% of cycles (vendor, 20,000+ brands, 2025-2026 blog) — [Recharge, "Supplement subscription retention: a leaky first reorder"](https://getrecharge.com/blog/supplement-subscription-retention/)
- Recurly benchmark across 1,900+ subscription sites: overall monthly churn 5.6% (B2B 4.91%, B2C 6.77%); voluntary 3.95% + involuntary 1.38%; Consumer Goods & Retail averages 6.5% monthly churn, "among the highest of any subscription industry"; B2C-heavy categories (Digital Media, Consumer Goods & Retail, Education) average 7.1% (vendor; Recurly's base is skewed to digital/SaaS, so consumer-goods sample is a minority) — [Recurly churn benchmarks](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/); [Recurly Consumer Goods report](https://recurly.com/resources/report/churn-benchmarks-consumer-goods/)
- A separate Recurly figure cited by aggregators gives average churn "about 3.27%, split roughly 2.41% voluntary and 0.86% involuntary" (vendor, older cohort; conflicts with the 5.6% figure above - different years/samples) — [shno.co, citing Recurly](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/subscription-retention-statistics)
- Aggregator ranges for 2026: DTC subscription ecommerce monthly churn 6.5-8.5%, "below 7% good, below 5% top quartile"; by category supplements/consumables 5-8%, coffee 5-10%, pet 6-10%, meal kits 8-15%, beauty boxes 8-14% (aggregator; no primary source per category) — [Everboost](https://everboost.co.uk/insights/dtc-subscription-churn-benchmarks/); similar ranges at [Finsi](https://www.finsi.ai/blog/ecommerce-churn-rate-benchmarks-2026/) and [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-subscription-churn-rate-by-category)
- "Anything under 6% monthly churn is solid for a consumer subscription, and under 3% puts you in the top quartile" (aggregator heuristic) — [SubJolt](https://www.subjolt.com/guides/churn-rate-benchmarks/)
- Ordergroove platform data: annual-prepay subscribers retain 2.5x longer than monthly subscribers at month 12 (28% vs 11% retained) (vendor data, restated by aggregator, 2025-2026) — [Eightx, citing Ordergroove](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-subscription-churn-by-billing-period-2026)
- Older reference point (2018, pre-dates scope): McKinsey survey found replenishment subscriptions retained more subscribers for a year or more than curation boxes (summary states 45% for replenishment, roughly ten points better than curation) and "more than a third of all subscribers canceled within three months" (independent survey, 2018; attribution via secondary summary) — [SubJolt](https://www.subjolt.com/guides/dtc-subscription-retention/); original at [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/thinking-inside-the-subscription-box-new-research-on-ecommerce-consumers)
- Pura (waterless smart diffuser + fragrance refill subscription, the closest public comparator) reports 300,000+ subscribers and >1 million households but publishes no churn figure; it states subscriptions "improved gross retention and predictable cash flows" — [Twilio customer story](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/pura); [Pura subscribe-and-save page](https://pura.com/pages/subscribe-and-save)

### Inferences
- A realistic planning band for a $40-50/month home-fragrance refill: 5-7% blended monthly churn (Recurly consumer goods 6.5%; Recharge H&W 4.2%), i.e., roughly 45-55% of a cohort gone by month 12 if churn is flat, or ~33% survival at 12 months per Recharge's merchant-wide curve (which front-loads churn).
- Home fragrance sits between beauty (lowest Recharge churn, 97.5% second-order conversion) and "home goods"; the beauty numbers are the most defensible proxy because purchase motivation (ritual, sensory, self-care) is similar and the swap-anytime scent catalog mirrors beauty's variety-seeking.
- The 12-month retention gap between small merchants (22%) and the platform average (33%) suggests operational maturity (onboarding, dunning, flexibility) explains roughly a third of retention variance, not just category.

### Gaps
- No source publishes a home-fragrance, candle, or diffuser-refill churn rate. Recharge's 2026 report has a "Home" vertical but its numbers were not visible in search summaries.
- Recharge's 2024 and 2025 State of Subscription Commerce headline churn tables (per-vertical monthly churn) could not be read directly; only secondary restatements were available.
- The 45%/33% six-/twelve-month retention figure could not be tied to a specific Recharge report year.

## Key Question 2: Involuntary vs voluntary churn share, and dunning recovery rates

### Takeaway
For consumer physical-goods subscriptions, involuntary (failed-payment) churn is typically 20-40% of total churn (Recharge health & wellness data implies ~29%: 1.2 of 4.2 points; Recurly's consumer-goods split implies ~25%), and median dunning programs recover about 50% of failed charges, with best-in-class at 60-85%.

### Cited Findings
- Recharge 2023 health & wellness churn ~4.2% monthly = ~3.0% voluntary + ~1.2% involuntary, i.e., involuntary is ~29% of churn (vendor, restated) — [Eightx, citing Recharge](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)
- Recurly: voluntary 3.95% vs involuntary 1.38% monthly (involuntary ~26% of total) across 1,900+ sites (vendor) — [Recurly churn benchmarks](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/)
- Loop (Shopify subscription platform, data from 1,000+ DTC brands): a failed payment the subscriber "never knew about" accounts for 20-40% of cancellations for DTC brands on Shopify; involuntary churn "can account for up to 53% of total subscriber churn across all merchant bases" (the 53% figure is Recurly's upper bound) (vendor) — [Loop, "Why subscribers cancel"](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/why-subscribers-cancel-save-offers-that-work)
- "For most consumer subscription brands, 60-75% of churn is voluntary ... and the rest is involuntary" (aggregator) — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ecommerce-subscription-retention-playbook-2026-churn-reduction)
- Churn Buster (dunning vendor for Shopify/Recharge/Loop/Skio): median recovery rate approximately 50% for Shopify merchants; top performers 60-70%+; teams "typically see 20%+ recovery rate improvements"; worked example assumes a 10% failed-payment rate on MRR (vendor) — [Churn Buster, Shopify dunning guide](https://churnbuster.io/articles/shopify-dunning); [Churn Buster, Shopify Subscriptions API](https://churnbuster.io/articles/shopify-subscriptions-api)
- Recurly: "median dunning recovers around half of failed charges, and best-in-class processes recover 70-85%"; consumer goods businesses on Recurly recovered more than $34 million in 2025 via automated recovery tools (vs $155M software, ~$100M digital media) (vendor, 2026 State of Subscriptions) — [Recurly 2026 State of Subscriptions](https://recurly.com/resources/report/state-of-subscriptions/); [Recurly failed-payment recovery blog](https://recurly.com/blog/failed-payment-recovery-data-based-strategy/)
- Recurly guidance: a well-optimized program should achieve a 10-20 point improvement over baseline retry performance; "if your current recovery rate is in the low-to-mid 50s, a rate in the high 60s to low 70s is an achievable target" (vendor) — [Recurly, failed payment recovery](https://recurly.com/blog/failed-payment-recovery-revenue-strategy/)
- Aggregator figures: dunning emails alone recover ~42% of failures; Stripe's recovery tooling averages ~57% of failed recurring payments; failed payments projected to cost subscription businesses $129B in 2025 (aggregators citing Stripe/Recurly; not independently verified) — [Digital Applied dunning playbook](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/failed-payment-recovery-dunning-playbook-2026); [Kaplan Group](https://www.kaplancollectionagency.com/news/subscription-facts-55-saas-and-b2b-payment-statistics-for-2025/)

### Inferences
- For a brand at ~6% monthly churn, ~1.5-2 points are likely involuntary; halving that with account-updater + smart retries + SMS/email dunning is worth roughly 0.75-1 point of monthly churn, the single cheapest retention gain available.
- At $40-50 AOV the failed-payment rate should sit toward the lower end of the 5-10% range typical of Shopify subscriptions, but a 30-day cycle means 12 charge attempts per year per subscriber, so card-expiry exposure is high.

### Gaps
- No source breaks out failed-payment rate or recovery rate specifically for beauty/home consumables on Shopify; all recovery benchmarks are cross-vertical.
- Recharge's own dunning-recovery benchmark (its native retry logic) was not found.

## Key Question 3: When churn concentrates in the lifecycle; retention curves after months 3, 6, 12

### Takeaway
Churn is heavily front-loaded: an independent 2026 analysis of ~40,000 cancellations at nine replenishment brands found 67% of cancellations occur by the second completed order, 78% by the third, and half within 90 days; Recharge's category curves show 5-13% of subscribers lost at the first reorder, then ~35-40% attrition per order through orders 2-4, flattening afterward.

### Cited Findings
- Everboost analysis of 39,766 cancellation events across nine DTC replenishment brands (Jan-Jul 2026): 78% of cancellations happened at or before the third completed order, 67% by the second, and half within 90 days of signup (independent agency analysis, UK-based firm, brand mix not disclosed) — [Everboost](https://everboost.co.uk/insights/dtc-subscription-churn-benchmarks/)
- Recharge (20,000+ brands): first-to-second-order conversion is 86.6% for supplements (the "leakiest" consumable), 92.9% health & wellness overall, 96.2% pet, 97.5% beauty, 99.3% coffee & tea; for supplements, 57.6% of subscribers reach order three, 33.8% order four, 9.8% order seven, 1.4% still active at order thirteen (vendor) — [Recharge, supplement retention](https://getrecharge.com/blog/supplement-subscription-retention/); [Eightx summary](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)
- Recharge merchant-wide survival: 45% active at 6 months, 33% at 12 months (vendor, restated) — [Darkroom, citing Recharge](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/subscription-churn)
- Ordergroove: monthly-billed subscribers retain 11% at month 12 vs 28% for annual prepay (vendor, restated) — [Eightx, citing Ordergroove](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-subscription-churn-by-billing-period-2026)
- "60-70% of subscribers are lost between order 1 and order 3 across most categories" and "a 22% drop-off rate occurs between the first and second orders placed on subscription for some DTC brands" (agency/aggregator commentary) — [Magnet Monster](https://magnetmonster.com/blog/how-to-reduce-dtc-subscription-churn-in-the-first-five-orders); [Platform DTC](https://platformdtc.com/answers/subscription-churn-rate-benchmarks)
- Loop: "the majority of churn happens in first 3 orders"; "70% of visits to portal lead to skip, pause, or cancellation" (vendor) — [Loop retention page](https://www.loopwork.co/retention); [Loop help center](https://help.loopwork.co/en/articles/14655237-subscription-retention-best-practices)
- Aggregator retention claims: "44% of subscription cancellations happen within the first 90 days"; "subscribers who reach day 90 have 85-90% long-term retention"; "25-40% of new subscribers churn before their second billing cycle" when onboarding is weak (aggregators, no primary source visible) — [Encomm](https://www.encomm.com/blogs/subscription-onboarding-first-90-days-churn-fix/); [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/ecommerce-customer-retention-90-day-guide/)
- A stat circulating as "roughly 61% of voluntary cancellations happen at the first two renewals, ~72% within the first four" appeared in a search summary attributed to Recharge's 2026 report, but a targeted search could not confirm the source; treat as unverified — no reliable URL
- Older (2018): McKinsey found more than a third of subscribers cancel within three months and over half within six (independent survey, pre-dates scope) — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/thinking-inside-the-subscription-box-new-research-on-ecommerce-consumers)

### Inferences
- Combining Recharge's curves, a beauty-like 30-day refill should expect ~97% to reach order 2, but then roughly 55-60% to reach order 3 and ~35% order 4 unless the second and third renewals are actively defended; the hazard rate roughly halves after order 4.
- For a 100ml refill on a 30-day cadence, the order-2/order-3 cliff coincides with the first moment a subscriber can see whether a bottle is actually finished, so "too much product" churn will surface at order 2-3, not order 1.

### Gaps
- No published month-by-month survival curve for beauty or home specifically beyond the second-order conversion figure.
- Everboost's nine-brand sample is not described (categories, price points, US vs UK share).

## Key Question 4: Top stated cancellation reasons for consumable subscriptions

### Takeaway
Loop's 2026 analysis of 1,000+ Shopify DTC brands is the only recent quantified breakdown found: price pressure 31%, overstock/"too much product" 16%, product fatigue 15%, changed needs 15%, with failed payments contributing a further 20-40% of total losses; structured cancel flows save ~15% of cancel attempts on average (20%+ for the best brands).

### Cited Findings
- Loop, data from 1,000+ DTC brands on Shopify (2026): subscribers cancel because of price pressure (31%), overstock (16%), product fatigue (15%), changed needs (15%), or a failed payment they never knew about (20-40%) (vendor; the percentages appear to be shares of voluntary cancel-flow reasons, with involuntary given separately) — [Loop, "Why subscribers cancel"](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/why-subscribers-cancel-save-offers-that-work)
- Loop: across 441,000+ cancel attempts analysed for 87 DTC brands, a structured three-stage cancellation funnel delivers 2.5x more saves than a generic discount; average save rate ~15% of cancel attempts, best-optimised brands 20%+; $3.9M MRR recovered (vendor) — [Loop, "Why subscribers cancel"](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/why-subscribers-cancel-save-offers-that-work)
- Recharge: "one of the most common reasons for customer churn is subscribers having too much product"; if customers cannot or do not know they can skip, change schedule, or try a new product they are likely to cancel (vendor, qualitative, no percentage) — [Recharge, "Why people cancel subscriptions"](https://getrecharge.com/blog/why-people-cancel-subscriptions-and-how-to-prevent-it-988b3d3b5d27/)
- Stay AI positions itself for "supplements, coffee, pet food, and other replenishment brands where 'too much product' is a top cancel reason" and auto-triggers a frequency change or skip when that reason is selected (vendor, qualitative) — [Loop overview of Stay AI](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/stay-ai-subscriptions-complete-guide-2026)
- Ordergroove published a dedicated overstock-retention piece ("Stop Losing Subscribers to Overstock") and a "disengaged subscription" concierge product, stating that only 4% of disengaged subscriptions would return if no action is taken (vendor) — [Ordergroove overstock blog](https://www.ordergroove.com/blog/stop-losing-subscribers-to-overstock-proven-retention-strategies-from-leading-brands/); [Ordergroove Disengaged Subscription Concierge FAQ](https://help.ordergroove.com/hc/en-us/articles/26078611500179-Disengaged-Subscription-Concierge-FAQ)
- General consumer survey (2022, all online subscriptions incl. media, Directions Research for Concepts Rise): among those likely to cancel, 43% cited "too costly", 20% consolidating services, 18% a promotional price/discount ending, 13% lack of quality (independent survey, older, not physical-goods-specific) — [Business Wire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20220301005436/en/No.-1-Reason-Why-Americans-to-Cancel-Online-Subscription-Services-in-2022-%E2%80%98Too-Costly%E2%80%99-Survey-Finds)

### Inferences
- For Maison Croyez, "overstock" and "product fatigue" (together ~31% in Loop's data) are the two reasons most directly addressable by product design: a 100ml bottle's actual burn-through time versus a fixed 30-day cadence determines overstock, and swap-anytime addresses fatigue only if subscribers know it exists and are prompted before the renewal charge.
- Price pressure at 31% argues for making the 25% subscription discount and cumulative savings visible at every renewal touchpoint rather than only at checkout.

### Gaps
- No cancellation-reason breakdown specific to beauty, home fragrance, or luxury price points was found.
- Recharge's own cancel-reason distribution (from its cancellation flow) was not visible in search results.
- "Forgot they subscribed" and "wanted to try once" were not quantified in any 2024-2026 source found.

## Key Question 5: Subscriber lifetime, LTV multiples vs one-time buyers, reactivation rates, and skip/swap/pause usage vs retention

### Takeaway
Recharge reports subscribers place nearly 3x more orders than one-time shoppers (a typical brand's subscribers placed 4.4 orders each in the trailing year, +15% YoY) and 53% of subscribers adjusted an order in the past year; Ordergroove's platform data says skip availability correlates with 135% longer subscriber lifetimes and swap with 71% longer; Recurly reports 75% of paused subscribers return to active billing and 20-25% of new subscriptions come from previously cancelled subscribers.

### Cited Findings
- Recharge Subscription Trend Report 2026 (20,000 brands): subscribers placed nearly 3x more orders than one-time shoppers and drove a growing share of revenue in every vertical (vendor) — [Recharge Subscription Trend Report 2026](https://getrecharge.com/reports/subscription-trend-report-2026/)
- Recharge "Subscriber Trends" report (Sept 17, 2024; 20,000 brands): a typical brand's subscribers placed 4.4 orders each over the past year, up 15% YoY; subscribers who start with one product end up subscribing to 4-6 overall; 53% of subscribers made order adjustments in the past year, up 3 points YoY; "one-time customers spend more up front, but subscribers' longevity quickly makes them more valuable customers" (vendor) — [PR Newswire / Recharge](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/recharge-subscriber-trends-insights-report-highlights-the-growing-value-of-subscribers-to-ecommerce-brands-302250623.html)
- Recharge 2023 State of Subscription Commerce (2022 data): 35% of subscribers made adjustments (skips, frequency changes, swaps); of those, 39% skipped an order (vendor, older) — [Recharge 2023 SOSC announcement](https://getrecharge.com/blog/announcing-recharges-interactive-2023-state-of-subscription-commerce-report/)
- Recharge: "active subscribers, even if their activity involves skipping a subscription, are stickier and generate a higher lifetime value"; supplements have the highest skip rate (6.9% of cycles) and second-lowest active churn (17.3%) (vendor) — [Recharge, supplement retention](https://getrecharge.com/blog/supplement-subscription-retention/)
- Recharge's latest State of Subscription Commerce, as restated by an aggregator: merchants offering skip, pause, swap and gift options see churn 32% lower than "cancel or keep" only (vendor via aggregator) — [Darkroom, citing Recharge](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/subscription-churn)
- Ordergroove internal data: subscribers stay 135% longer when they can skip an order and 71% longer when they can swap a product (vendor, correlational, year not stated; repeated across Ordergroove guides) — [Ordergroove, understanding subscriber churn](https://www.ordergroove.com/blog/understanding-subscriber-churn/); [Ordergroove subscription optimization guide](https://www.ordergroove.com/guides/subscription-program-optimization/)
- Ordergroove: only 4% of "disengaged" subscriptions return with no intervention (vendor) — [Ordergroove Disengaged Subscription Concierge FAQ](https://help.ordergroove.com/hc/en-us/articles/26078611500179-Disengaged-Subscription-Concierge-FAQ)
- Recurly network benchmarks (2025-2026): businesses adding "pause before cancel" saw a 337% surge in pause usage, and 75% of paused subscribers eventually return to active billing; separately, a 46% reactivation rate after pause is cited for pause offered inside the cancellation flow; 1 in 4 new subscriptions come from a previously cancelled subscriber (another Recurly page says 20% of new acquisitions in 2025 are returning subscribers) (vendor, cross-industry, mostly digital) — [Recurly 2026 State of Subscriptions](https://recurly.com/resources/report/2026-state-of-subscriptions/); [Recurly winback blog](https://recurly.com/blog/customer-winback-strategies-for-subscriptions/); [Recurly pause blog](https://recurly.com/blog/why-pausing-a-subscription-can-be-a-powerful-retention-tactic/)
- Aggregator restatements: "skip-a-month typically retains 80%+ of users; open-ended pauses retain about 40-50%; 3 of 4 subscribers who pause eventually return; subscriptions offering skip/pause/swap/frequency churn 15-30% less than rigid ones" (aggregator; the 3-of-4 figure is Recurly's) — [Mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/subscription-churn-retention-benchmarks-statistics/); [Loop flexibility playbook](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/subscription-flexibility-pause-skip-swap-reduce-shopify-churn)
- Ordergroove 2025 State of Direct Selling Subscriptions survey: 53% of leaders say retention is their top subscription challenge; only 15% have full visibility into churn and LTV; most programs still rely on discounts plus pause/skip, few offer swaps, delivery flexibility or prepaid packages (vendor survey of direct-selling brands) — [Ecommerce Fastlane, citing Ordergroove](https://ecommercefastlane.com/unlocking-growth-in-direct-selling-subscriptions-5-insights-from-ordergrooves-2025-survey/)

### Inferences
- Implied average subscriber lifetime: at 5-6.5% flat monthly churn, mean lifetime is ~15-20 months, but the front-loaded real curve (33% at 12 months) means the median subscriber lasts closer to 4-6 months while a long tail lifts the mean; Recharge's 4.4 orders/subscriber/year is consistent with a median of roughly 4-5 renewals.
- The correlation between skip usage and retention is partly selection (engaged subscribers skip instead of cancelling), so the actionable lever is prompting the skip/swap before the charge, not merely having the feature.
- Recurly's 75% pause-return figure is dominated by digital subscriptions; for a physical refill where the subscriber still has product on the shelf, pause behaves like a delayed skip and the return rate should be similar or higher, but no physical-goods pause-return benchmark was found.

### Gaps
- No published LTV-to-CAC or LTV multiple (subscriber vs one-time) specific to beauty or home; Recharge gives order-count multiples (~3x), not dollar LTV.
- No physical-goods reactivation/win-back rate benchmark; Recurly's 20-25% "returning subscriber" share is cross-industry.
- Share of subscribers who use swap specifically (as opposed to skip) was not published.

## Key Question 6: Subscription discount depth (10/15/20/25%) vs retention or acquisition

### Takeaway
No vendor publishes controlled data linking discount depth to retention; the consistent practitioner guidance (Intelligems, Nord Media, Canopy for Amazon S&S) is that 10-20% is the normal ongoing subscribe-and-save band, deeper discounts lift take rate but attract faster-churning deal-seekers, and retention should be measured at 30 and 90 days before increasing depth.

### Cited Findings
- Intelligems (Shopify price-testing tool): "a 15% discount that retains customers for 8 months beats a 25% discount with 4-month retention"; higher discounts convert better initially but may attract deal-seekers who churn faster; recommends A/B testing subscribe & save depth on subscription orders/revenue/profit per visitor (vendor guidance, no aggregate test results) — [Intelligems, pricing subscription plans](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/blog/how-should-i-price-subscription-plans)
- Nord Media: subscription discounts "typically 10 to 20 percent"; a 20% code on a 45% gross-margin product "costs you nearly half the profit on that order" (agency guidance) — [Nord Media](https://nordmedia.com/blog-posts/subscription-pricing-strategy)
- Illustrative claim: "the 20% discount group might convert 30% better initially but churn 50% faster"; tests need 4-6 weeks minimum before retention differences appear (agency, hypothetical framing) — [Growth Suite](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-discount/percentage-off-discounts-guide/optimal-discount-rate)
- Amazon Subscribe & Save context (not DTC, but consumables): case examples of a pet brand using 20% first-order + 10% ongoing achieving 73% retention at 90 days with 36% of revenue on subscription; a beauty brand at 5% seller-funded ongoing achieving 2x S&S penetration; guidance to start at 5%, measure 30- and 90-day retention, benchmark 90%+ retention at 30 days and 75%+ at 90 days (agency case studies) — [Canopy Management](https://canopymanagement.com/amazon-subscribe-save-supplement-brands-retention/)
- Acquisition-offer depth and repeat: "customers who arrive via a heavy first-order discount, a giveaway, or a marketplace deal site repeat at a fraction of the rate of customers acquired on full price, and the gap shows up in the 90-day cohort curve within one quarter"; segmenting by first-order discount depth "explains more retention variance than anything happening inside the ESP" (agency) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/ecommerce-customer-retention-90-day-guide/)
- Practitioner note that at the offer stage of cancellation flows, "subscription pauses took precedence over discounts", i.e., flexibility saves outperform discount saves (agency) — [Chargebee retention cheat sheet](https://www.chargebee.com/blog/subscription-ecommerce-customer-retention-insights/)
- A raw dog food brand that restructured its offer to hold the acquisition discount through order two and reward continuity cut subscriber churn 13% and upcoming-order churn 61.7% (agency case study) — [Magnet Monster](https://magnetmonster.com/blog/how-to-reduce-dtc-subscription-churn-in-the-first-five-orders)

### Inferences
- A flat 25% ongoing discount is at the deep end of the published 10-20% norm; the data suggests the depth buys take-rate more than retention, so Maison Croyez should measure whether 25% subscribers retain better than a 15-20% test cell before treating 25% as fixed.
- Case evidence points toward a "front-loaded then step-down" or "loyalty-escalating" structure (e.g., deeper discount or gift at orders 2-3) outperforming a flat deep discount, because the risk window is orders 2-3.

### Gaps
- No Recharge, Ordergroove, Loop, Skio or Stay AI publication with aggregate retention by discount tier was found; the 20%-converts-30%-better/churns-50%-faster figure is illustrative, not measured.
- No luxury-price-point data on discount sensitivity.

## Key Question 7: First-order experience, onboarding and second-order timing vs 90-day retention

### Takeaway
Quantified primary data is thin: the strongest evidence is structural (Recharge's per-category first-reorder conversion of 86.6-99.3% and Everboost's finding that half of cancellations occur inside 90 days), plus Loop's finding that 70% of portal visits end in skip, pause or cancel. Aggregator claims that post-purchase email programs cut 90-day churn ~14% and that a week-2 touchpoint lifts 6-month retention ~9% could not be traced to a primary study.

### Cited Findings
- Recharge: first-to-second-order conversion ranges from 86.6% (supplements) to 97.5% (beauty) and 99.3% (coffee & tea); brands with a "leaky first reorder" still retain later cohorts normally once subscribers clear order two (vendor) — [Recharge, supplement retention](https://getrecharge.com/blog/supplement-subscription-retention/)
- Everboost: half of cancellations within 90 days; 67% by the second completed order (independent, 2026) — [Everboost](https://everboost.co.uk/insights/dtc-subscription-churn-benchmarks/)
- Loop: upcoming-order emails are a primary lever because the majority of churn is in the first three orders and 70% of portal visits lead to skip, pause or cancel (vendor) — [Loop, upcoming order emails](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/reduce-dtc-subscription-churn-upcoming-order-emails); [Loop retention page](https://www.loopwork.co/retention)
- Loop: founder-led video in onboarding/cancel flows reduces cancellations (vendor, qualitative case studies) — [Loop, founder videos](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/dtc-founders-reduce-subscription-cancellations)
- Aggregator claims (no primary source visible): poor onboarding increases 90-day churn by 30%; automated post-purchase emails (order + tips + support) reduce 90-day churn by 14%; a second-week onboarding touchpoint boosts 6-month retention by 9%; average subscription retention 31% vs top performers 45-55% attributed to structured post-purchase programs; half of all reorders happen within 30 days of first purchase and more than three-quarters within 90 days — [Encomm](https://www.encomm.com/blogs/subscription-onboarding-first-90-days-churn-fix/); [SundaySky onboarding statistics](https://sundaysky.com/blog/customer-onboarding-statistics/); [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/ecommerce-customer-retention-90-day-guide/)
- Pura (home fragrance comparator) states it detects device connectivity issues and proactively reaches out with troubleshooting "before frustration leads to churn", and personalizes fragrance suggestions to keep subscribers "excited about their subscription instead of pausing or canceling" (vendor case study) — [Twilio customer story on Pura](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/pura)
- Stay AI case study: a beverage brand's month-2 churn fell from ~60% to ~30%; OLIPOP saw a 26% churn reduction after migration (vendor case studies, not benchmarks) — [Stay AI vs Skio](https://stay.ai/stay-vs-skio/)

### Inferences
- For a device-plus-refill model, the first 30 days carry two failure modes that beauty does not: hardware setup friction (Pura's stated churn trigger) and mismatch between refill volume and the 30-day cadence. Both should be addressed before the first renewal charge, when the order-2 decision is effectively made.
- Because Recharge's beauty first-reorder rate is 97.5%, a home-fragrance brand seeing materially lower order-2 conversion should suspect device/onboarding or cadence problems rather than category norms.

### Gaps
- No controlled study quantifying the effect of a specific onboarding sequence or second-order timing on 90-day retention for physical subscriptions; all such percentages found are unsourced aggregator claims.
- No published data on optimal first-renewal timing (e.g., 30 vs 45 days) for consumables.

## Metrics That Matter Most (synthesis for the strategy)

### Takeaway
Across sources, five metrics recur as the leading indicators for a monthly consumable subscription: (1) order-2 and order-3 conversion (where 67-78% of cancellations occur), (2) involuntary churn share and dunning recovery rate (20-40% of churn, ~50% median recovery), (3) skip/swap engagement rate (53% of Recharge subscribers adjust orders; skip correlates with 135% longer lifetime per Ordergroove), (4) cancel-flow save rate (~15% average, 20%+ best), and (5) 6- and 12-month cohort survival (45% / 33% Recharge merchant-wide).

### Cited Findings
- See Key Questions 1-5 above for each figure and source; the composite is an inference from those sources.

### Inferences
- A brand tracking only blended monthly churn will miss that most losses are decided by the third charge; cohort-by-order survival (order-2, order-3, order-4 conversion) is the operating metric, with monthly churn as the lagging output.

### Gaps
- No single source ranks metrics by predictive power for physical-goods subscriptions; the ranking above is synthesized.
