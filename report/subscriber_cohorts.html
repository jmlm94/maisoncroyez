Read-out

  • About six in ten pay the first renewal on time. 80 of 128 subscribers who were 7 days past their first due date had paid (62%). The worst cohort is Jul 20 (39%), the best is the Aug 10 build-your-kit week (73%, 27 of 37). Jul 27 sits at 62%.
  • The second renewal is where the base thins out. Only the launch cohort is far enough along: 5 of 11 paid renewal 2 (45%). The Jul 27 and August cohorts reach that point through October, which is when this table becomes decisive.
  • 45-day plans are not retaining better. 50% paid renewal 1 (9 of 18) against 65% on 30-day plans, and they bill less often, so they produce less cash per subscriber per month on both counts.
  • Multi-scent subscribers renew at least as well as singles (65% vs 62%) while paying about twice as much per delivery, which is why the 2- and 3-scent tiers are the ones to push.
  • Almost none of the drop-off is cancellations. Subi shows 5 real cancellations. The rest of the missed renewals are cards that failed and were never recovered, so the curve above is mostly a payment-recovery curve.
How to use this. Treat renewal 1 at about 62% and renewal 2 at roughly 45% as today's baseline when pricing an offer. If Subi recovery lifts renewal 1 to 80%, every cohort row shifts up and the payback math in the offer guide shortens by about a month.

Data as of September 25, 2026 from Shopify orders and Subi customer tags. Renewal due dates are computed from the first order date and the plan cadence; Subi's own billing calendar may differ by a day or two.
