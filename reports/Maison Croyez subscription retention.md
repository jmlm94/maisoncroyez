# Win the Third Bottle: Maison Croyez Subscription Retention

Prepared for Jose Lepage, CEO. Research date 2026-09-20. Based on six research notes (benchmarks, competitor teardown, product and offer design, psychology and legal, tactics and tooling, plus the internal brand context file).

## Summary and the one recommendation

Most subscribers who leave a monthly consumable subscription leave before their third bottle ships. An independent 2026 analysis of about 40,000 cancellations at nine replenishment brands found 67% of cancellations happen by the second completed order, 78% by the third, and half inside 90 days. Platform-wide data from Recharge shows only 45% of subscribers still active at six months and 33% at twelve. The reasons are consistent across every source: product piling up, a card that quietly failed, boredom with the same product, and price. For Maison Croyez, three of those four reasons are designable before launch, because the subscription does not exist in Shopify yet.

The one recommendation: do not launch a flat 30-day auto-ship. Launch a usage-matched subscription, and spend the entire launch retention budget on getting each subscriber from bottle one to bottle three. In practice that means four things, in this order.

- Ask two questions at signup (which timer, which mode) and set the shipping cadence from the answer, with 30, 45 and 60 day options. A 100ml bottle only lasts about 30 days in a narrow band of use. Outside that band, a fixed 30-day cadence ships too much oil to light users and too little to heavy users. Both outcomes end in a cancel.
- Send a reminder three to five days before every charge, by SMS and email, with one-tap links to skip, move the date, or swap the scent. Pura does this and it is the single cheapest defence against the "surprise charge" complaint that dominates the category's reviews.
- Make cancelling fully self-serve online, with one honest save screen that offers skip, cadence change, swap and a self-resuming pause. No discount on that screen. This fits the brand's "never compete on price" rule, it is where the law is heading, and the data says pause and skip save more people than discounts anyway.
- Install Loop (Starter) or Recharge (Standard) as the subscription engine and build seven Klaviyo flows on its events. The bible already lists five of these flows as required and missing.

Everything else in this report (milestone gifts, a subscriber warranty extension, prepaid plans, win-back, loyalty) matters, but it matters after bottle three. Get the first 90 days right and the six and twelve month numbers largely follow.

The 25% discount is locked, so this report does not propose changing it at launch. It does flag it as a decision for you: 25% with no commitment is deeper than any direct competitor's no-strings plan (Pura runs 20%), and the practitioner evidence says depth buys signups more than it buys loyalty.

## How to read the figures in this report

The research environment blocked direct page loads for almost every source. Every external number below therefore came from a search engine's excerpt of the cited page, not from a full read of the document. Headline figures are reliable; exact definitions (for example how a platform counts "churn") could not be checked. Each figure is tagged by source type.

- Vendor: a subscription platform's own customer data or marketing (Recharge, Loop, Ordergroove, Recurly, Skio, Stay AI, Churn Buster, Klaviyo, Smile).
- Aggregator: a third-party blog restating vendor numbers.
- Independent: an academic study, a court or regulator, a consumer survey, or an agency analysing its own client dataset.
- Unverified: a figure that could not be traced to any primary source. These are noted but not relied on.

Brand facts (prices, costs, offer stack, live store state) come from the internal brand bible and live Shopify and Klaviyo reads on 2026-09-20 and are treated as ground truth.

## The subscription is won or lost by the third bottle

Start with what normal looks like. Consumer physical-goods subscriptions run roughly 5 to 8% monthly churn. Recurly's consumer goods benchmark is 6.5% monthly across 1,900+ sites ([Recurly](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/), vendor). Recharge's health and wellness merchants ran about 4.2% in 2023 ([Eightx citing Recharge](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark), vendor via aggregator). Across Recharge's merchant base, **45% of subscribers are still active at six months and 33% at twelve** ([Darkroom citing Recharge](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/subscription-churn), vendor via aggregator). Small merchants do worse: Recharge's 2023 review found retention at smaller brands "falling to a shaky 22% at the 12-month mark" while mid-sized brands improved ([PR Newswire, Recharge](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/recharge-releases-2023-in-review-the-year-of-the-routine-insights-report-302054874.html), vendor). Both groups sell in the same categories, so the gap between 22% and 33% comes from operations: onboarding, dunning, flexibility. That is the part Maison Croyez controls.

No source publishes a home fragrance churn rate. The closest proxy is beauty, and it is a favourable one. Recharge reports Beauty and Personal Care as the lowest-churn vertical, with a **97.5% first-to-second-order conversion**, against 92.9% for health and wellness and 86.6% for supplements ([Recharge Subscription Trend Report 2026](https://getrecharge.com/reports/subscription-trend-report-2026/), vendor). Purchase motivation in home fragrance (ritual, sensory pleasure, self-care) is closer to beauty than to vitamins. A planning band of 5 to 7% blended monthly churn is realistic, and anything under 5% would put the program in the top quartile by aggregator heuristics ([Everboost](https://everboost.co.uk/insights/dtc-subscription-churn-benchmarks/), aggregator).

The shape of the curve matters more than the average. Churn is front-loaded. Everboost's analysis of **39,766 cancellations across nine replenishment brands (January to July 2026) found 78% of cancellations at or before the third completed order, 67% by the second, and half within 90 days of signup** ([Everboost](https://everboost.co.uk/insights/dtc-subscription-churn-benchmarks/), independent agency analysis; brand mix not disclosed). Recharge's supplement curve shows the same cliff: 57.6% of subscribers reach order three, 33.8% reach order four, and the hazard roughly halves after that ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/blog/supplement-subscription-retention/), vendor). Skio's June 2026 post puts it bluntly: 70 to 80% of supplement subscribers churn before order three, the best brands keep 50 to 60% past it, and a first cancel attempt inside 60 days "means your onboarding is broken" ([Skio](https://skio.com/blog/why-70-of-supplement-subscribers-churn-after-order-2), vendor).

Think of it like a new gym membership. Almost nobody cancels in week one. The cancel happens in month two or three, when the routine has not formed and the charge shows up again. The subscription business is a routine business. Recharge titled its year-in-review "The Year of the Routine" for that reason ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/resources/2023-in-review/), vendor).

For a 100ml bottle on a 30-day cycle there is a specific reason the cliff lands at bottle two and three: that is the first moment the customer can see whether bottle one is actually empty. If it is half full, bottle two arriving feels like waste. If it ran out in ten days, bottle two arriving feels late and the diffuser has sat silent for two weeks. Either way the second charge is the decision point. The order-2 and order-3 conversion rates, not blended monthly churn, are the operating metrics for the first six months.

Why subscribers leave is consistent across sources. Loop's analysis of 1,000+ Shopify brands breaks voluntary cancellations into **price pressure 31%, overstock 16%, product fatigue 15%, changed needs 15%**, with failed payments adding a further 20 to 40% of total losses on top ([Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/why-subscribers-cancel-save-offers-that-work), vendor). Recharge ranks the reasons as "stopped using the product, the payment failed, or the value stopped justifying the price, in roughly that order" ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/resources/2023-in-review/), vendor). Ordergroove adds that "up to 19% of cancellations happen because subscribers don't realize they can adjust the timing, contents, or frequency" ([Ordergroove](https://www.ordergroove.com/blog/from-cancel-to-keep-best-practices-that-retain-subscribers/), vendor). Overstock, fatigue and not-knowing-you-can-adjust together account for roughly half of voluntary churn, and all three are design problems rather than pricing problems.

## A flat 30-day cadence ships the wrong amount to most customers

The bible says a 100ml bottle "lasts 30 days regular use." The category's own published figures show why a single number cannot be right for everyone. Every waterless brand quotes a lifespan that depends on intensity and hours per day, and the real range is two to four times wide. Aroma360 states its 120ml bottles last 30 to 60 days at 6 to 8 hours daily on medium, 60 to 90 days on low, and 20 to 40 days on high ([Aroma360](https://aroma360.com/collections/diffuser-oil), vendor). AromaTech says a 120ml bottle lasts up to four weeks at eight hours a day on its AroMini ([AromaTech](https://www.facebook.com/aromatech/photos/were-often-asked-how-long-does-a-bottle-of-aromatech-diffuser-oil-last-running-y/2193224674179386/), vendor). Hotel Collection says about one month at 4 to 8 hours a day ([Hotel Collection](https://www.hotelcollection.com/pages/faqs), vendor). Pura's own materials disagree with each other (120 hours on packaging, 350 hours on a help page) and independent reviewers measure two to three weeks on high, four to six on medium, eight to ten on low ([My Balanced Space](https://mybalancedspace.com/pura-4-review/), independent review).

Working backwards from those claims gives a planning band of roughly 0.3 to 0.6 ml per hour for a cold-air unit on medium. This is derived from stated bottle life and schedules, not from any manufacturer specification. Applied to the Maison Croyez diffuser and its four timers, at 0.5 ml per hour a 100ml bottle is about 200 diffusion hours:

- 12-hour timer: about 17 days.
- 6-hour timer: about 33 days.
- 3-hour timer: about 67 days.
- 1-hour timer: several months.
- On the G3 high mode (25 seconds on, 60 off) the draw is likely two to three times the G1 mode, so a 12-hour high user could run dry in eight to ten days.

The "30 days" claim only holds for roughly a 6-hour medium user. A bedroom-only evening user on the 3-hour timer will have a bottle and a half on the shelf when refill two arrives. A living room 12-hour user will have been without scent for two weeks. The category literature names the consequence: "when you ship faster than consumption, you create inventory pile-up, guilt, skip behavior, and eventually cancellations" ([RetentionX](https://newsletter.retentionx.com/p/your-subscription-is-too-fast), practitioner). Pura's own gap between marketed refill life and real life shows up in its complaints as "refills add up" and vials accumulating ([WeTried.it](https://wetried.it/is-pura-worth-it/), independent review). Aera's shows up as "cartridges didn't last as long as advertised" ([Honest Brand Reviews](https://www.honestbrandreviews.com/reviews/aera-diffuser-review/), review aggregator).

The fix is cheap because the diffuser already has the inputs. Three questions at subscription signup ("Which timer will you mostly use? Which mode? How many rooms?") map directly to a 30, 45 or 60 day cadence, or to two bottles every 60 days. No home fragrance brand was found doing this; the precedent comes from pet supplements and coffee, where a quiz "does the cadence math the customer would otherwise skip" ([RevenueHunt](https://revenuehunt.com/product-quiz-subscriptions/), vendor). The category incumbents already offer wider cadence menus: AromaTech one to four months, Pura one to three months, Scentiment defaults to every three months ([AromaTech](https://aromatechscent.com/pages/subscription-scenting); [Scentiment](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/faqs), vendor pages). Offering only 30 days would make Maison Croyez the least flexible plan in the set.

Two further consequences follow. First, the acquisition offer ("30 days of fragrance free with every diffuser") has the same problem: the free bottle's real life is 10 to 60 days, so the first paid shipment date should be set from the cadence answer, not fixed at day 30. Second, the planned 50ml bottle is best positioned as the light-user and second-room format rather than as a cheaper price rung. When a subscriber says "too much product," the right answer is a longer cadence or the smaller bottle, never a discount ([Appstle](https://appstle.com/blog/shopify-subscription-cadence-mismatch/), vendor).

Before launch, run a bench test. Put a full 100ml bottle on a diffuser at each mode and timer combination and log days to empty. That takes about three weeks, costs a few bottles, and replaces every estimate above with a real table. It also gives Patricia an honest line for the product page ("about 30 days on the 6-hour timer, about two months on the 3-hour timer") that no competitor currently offers.

## Her nose stops noticing the scent before the second bottle arrives

There is a physiological reason fragrance subscriptions lose people at month two that coffee and vitamins do not have. Continuous exposure to an odorant in subjects' own homes for two weeks "produced an odorant-specific reduction in sensitivity and perceived intensity," and the reduced sensitivity "was still evident up to 2 weeks following the last exposure" ([PubMed 8710455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8710455/), independent, peer-reviewed). Short-term adaptation to an ambient scent sets in within about 15 to 20 minutes ([Hotel Collection](https://www.hotelcollection.com/blogs/news/the-ultimate-antidote-to-nose-blindness), vendor). Aera's reviews already contain the churn version of this: "some customers become accustomed to the aromas too quickly" ([Trustpilot Aera](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.aeraforhome.com), consumer reviews). Pura app reviews say "scents might start to feel repetitive" ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1215372874/pura-smart-fragrance-dispenser/reviews), consumer reviews).

The practical risk is simple. A subscriber runs Peace continuously for four weeks. By week three she no longer smells it. She concludes the oil is weak or the diffuser stopped working. The second charge arrives and she cancels for "value." Nothing was wrong with the product. It is like a clock ticking in the hallway: after a week you only hear it when it stops.

Three things counter it, and all three are already in the brand's hands.

- Intermittent diffusion. A vehicle fragrance patent notes that "intermittently blowing scent restrains olfactory adaptation that would occur in continuously blowing" ([USPTO 9,914,341](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9914341), independent). The diffuser's three cycle modes and timers are exactly this. Onboarding should teach a schedule (for example the 3-hour timer at bedtime) rather than "leave it on."
- Rotation. Aroma360 organizes its oils for "a 2-4 week rotation" to "prevent long-term sensory adaptation" ([Aroma360](https://aroma360.com/blogs/aroma360-insights/why-a-home-scent-machine-is-the-ultimate-antidote-to-nose-blindness), vendor). Because adaptation persists about two weeks after exposure stops, alternating scents month to month makes the signature scent feel new again on its return. This is a stronger argument for swap in home fragrance than in any other category.
- Education. Content that explains why the scent seems to fade converts a churn trigger into a demonstration of expertise. AG1 uses the same move: when the benefit is hard to feel, explain the mechanism ([Rachel MacPherson](https://rachelmacpherson.ca/why-wellness-brands-lose-customers/), practitioner). A day-20 message in the brand's voice does the work: "Has your scent gone quiet? That is your nose adapting, not the oil. Try switching rooms for a day, or let the next bottle be a different intention."

Rotation must be opt-in, not forced. The academic record is split on variety: in subscription boxes, perceived variety raises continuance intention ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3485768.3485772), independent), but in services generally, variety-seeking "negatively affects customer retention" ([Berné et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222773169_The_effect_of_variety-seeking_on_customer_retention_in_services), independent). Trade Coffee found "most users don't want to try random new things" ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/we-dont-want-to-be-a-coffee-of-the-month-club-how-subscription-coffee-service-trade-is-planning-for-post-pandemic-retention/), independent press). The design that reconciles both is anchored rotation: she names a signature scent (identity, consistency), and the subscription offers to alternate it with one seasonal or mood scent (novelty, adaptation reset). Variety inside the brand retains; variety she has to go elsewhere for churns.

This is also where the intention positioning earns its keep. No direct competitor ties retention to the emotional use case; every mechanic in the category is transactional (points, discounts, gifts). A monthly intention prompt that arrives with each refill is an external trigger in the Hook Model sense ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/blog/the-hook-model), practitioner summary), and it lands at exactly the moment she is most adapted to the prior scent. Qualitative wellness research finds people stay loyal to products with "visible, tangible forms," to brands that "communicate care rather than just efficacy," and to routines with "immediate sensory feedback" ([Indeemo](https://indeemo.com/blog/consumer-health-wellness-research), independent qualitative). A plug-in diffuser is passive, so the ritual has to be a deliberate human moment: choosing the scent, setting the intention, switching it on. Design the refill card and the onboarding around that moment.

What does not fit the brand: streaks, countdowns and loss-framed nudges. Streaks work through loss aversion and the same sources describe them as engineering "obligation" ([Design Bootcamp](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/streaks-and-daily-rewards-as-habit-forming-systems-dab7f5a34539), practitioner). No evidence was found that they work for a 30+ luxury audience, and in a cancellation flow they are legally risky. Build switching cost instead through owned things: a named signature scent, a saved schedule per room, a scent history, an anniversary note. These raise the cost of leaving without pressure.

## Competitors lock people in; Maison Croyez wins by making leaving easy

The direct competitor set splits into two models. The first is honest subscribe-and-save at 10 to 20% with no commitment: Aera 15% first order then 10%, Scentiment 10%, Aroma360 Scent Club 10%, AromaTech 20%, Pura 20% ([Aera](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/subscriptions); [Scentiment](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/subscription); [Aroma360](https://aroma360.com/pages/scent-club); [AromaTech](https://aromatechscent.com/pages/subscription-scenting); [Pura](https://pura.com/pages/subscribe-and-save), vendor pages). The second is a free or discounted device against a locked subscription, with the device price clawed back on early cancel: Pura 12 months on its free-device offer, Hotel Collection three to six months, Aroma360 90 days with a $99 early termination fee, Drift six months ([Pura Discounted Diffuser Terms](https://pura.com/pages/discounted-diffuser-terms-and-conditions); [Hotel Collection](https://www.hotelcollection.com/pages/faqs); [Aroma360 Cancellation Policy](https://aroma360.com/policies/subscription-policy); [Drift FAQs](https://drift.co/pages/faqs), vendor pages).

The second model generates the category's complaint record. Hotel Collection sits at 1.1 on Trustpilot from 601 reviews and Aroma360 at 1.2, with "unwanted subscriptions" and unreachable support the recurring themes ([Trustpilot Hotel Collection](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/hotelcollection.com); [Trustpilot Aroma360](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/aroma360.com), consumer reviews). Aroma360 customers "who attempted to cancel due to equipment failures were told they could cancel but would still be responsible for early termination fees" ([BBB Aroma360](https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/miami/profile/essential-oils/aroma360-llc-0633-90405374/complaints), consumer complaints). A Pura BBB complaint alleges the promo period was changed "from 6 months to 12 months" mid-term ([BBB Pura](https://www.bbb.org/us/ut/pleasant-grove/profile/air-fragrance/pura-1166-90023918/complaints), consumer complaints). TikTok has a discovery page titled "How to Cancel Aroma 360 Subscription," which is a measure of search demand for cancellation help ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-to-cancel-aroma-360-subscription)).

Because Maison Croyez sells the diffuser at full price, it has no device subsidy to claw back and therefore no reason for a commitment. That is a marketable difference: "No lock-in. No clawback. Cancel in two clicks from your account." Every direct competitor that gives away hardware cannot say it. The claim is only credible if three things are true at launch: no pre-checked subscription box at checkout (the auto-enrolment surprise is Drift's and Scentiment's top complaint), cancellation fully self-serve in the account portal (Scentiment's "cancel link doesn't work" complaint), and no charges after a confirmed cancel ([Trustpilot Drift](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/drift.co); [Trustpilot Scentiment](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/scentiment.com), consumer reviews).

The law now points the same way. The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025 on procedural grounds, days before it took effect ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/07/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-negative-option-rule), law firm). The FTC restarted rulemaking with an advance notice in March 2026 and continues to enforce under ROSCA and Section 5 ([Covington, March 2026](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/ftc-launches-new-rulemaking-on-the-negative-option-rule), law firm). 2026 cases include Shutterstock ($35 million, May), the Genesis Tech "subscription trap" enterprise (June), and Hims & Hers (July, joined by Utah and Los Angeles County) ([Troutman](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/05/ftc-targets-shutterstocks-negative-option-subscriptions-in-35-million-settlement/); [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-sues-stop-sprawling-enterprise-operating-unlawful-subscription-schemes); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/ftc-lawsuit-hims-hers-rosca), regulator and law firm). Hims & Hers is the relevant precedent: a polished premium DTC experience did not protect it.

State law is the operative standard for a US brand, and California is the de facto national rule. Under AB 2863 (effective July 1, 2025): consent to auto-renewal must be its own separate step and "can no longer be bundled into general terms-of-service acceptance or buried behind a pre-checked box"; the law now covers free-to-paid conversions; cancellation must be available "through the same method they used to subscribe"; an annual reminder of terms and cancellation instructions is required; and proof of consent must be kept three years ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect), law firm). Minnesota adds an annual notice regardless of term and "uniquely limits the save offers you can show" ([Churnkey](https://churnkey.co/guides/state-automatic-renewal-laws), vendor guide; [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/states-press-forward-with-automatic-renewal-laws-amidst-vacating-of-the-ftcs-negative-option-rule/), law firm). Massachusetts requires online cancellation on the same website used to sign up ([ADventures in Law](https://www.adventures-in-law.com/blogs/automatic-renewal-law-updates-massachusetts-new-york-and-connecticut/), law firm). Designing to California plus Minnesota covers most of the map. Exact day-counts for the notice windows could not be verified from statute text this session and should be confirmed by counsel before launch.

Two points matter specifically for the Maison Croyez offer stack. First, the "30 days of fragrance free with every diffuser" offer, if it starts a subscription, is a free-to-paid conversion. California requires separate consent and advance notice of the conversion, and card network rules require a reminder "between 3 to 7 days before the end of a free trial period" with cancellation instructions ([Chargeback Gurus](https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/mastercard-subscription-rules), payments consultancy summary; exact windows differ between Visa and Mastercard and should be confirmed). Second, network rules require that customers "must be able to cancel their subscriptions online, even if they didn't enroll online," and that a link to a general customer service page "is not acceptable" as cancellation information ([Chargeback Gurus](https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/mastercard-subscription-rules)). Non-compliance here shows up as chargebacks, not lawsuits.

What to adopt from the better-run programs: Pura's alert "3 days before your subscription processes so you have time to swap fragrances, change the delivery date, or cancel" ([Pura](https://pura.com/pages/subscribe-and-save), vendor); Pura's skip that "will push it back for 4 weeks" and then resumes on its own ([Pura Help](https://help.pura.com/en/Account_Subs/Skip-a-Subscription-40b5), vendor); Scentiment's tenure gifts ("FREE diffuser oil on your 3rd, 6th, 9th subscription order plus FREE Diffuser Mini 2 LE on your 13th") and price lock ([Scentiment](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/subscription), vendor); and the subscriber warranty extension both Pura and Scentiment use (Pura extends its one-year warranty "through the lifespan of your subscription") ([Pura Support](https://help.pura.com/en/orders_shipping/Diffuser-Warranty-Policy), vendor). Device failure is the biggest churn trigger in the category's complaint record; a warranty that lasts while subscribed converts that churn event into a retention benefit at no cost until a failure occurs.

## Build the save screen around skip and swap, and keep the discount out of it

When a subscriber clicks cancel, the consensus practice is: ask the reason first, then branch. Loop's analysis of 441,000+ cancel attempts across 87 brands found a structured reason-based flow "delivers 2.5x more saves than a generic discount," with an average save rate of about 15% and the best brands above 20% ([Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/why-subscribers-cancel-save-offers-that-work), vendor). Recharge's pause feature alone averages a 9.6% save rate across 1,700+ merchants, and three of four pausers return within months ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/blog/reduce-your-cancellations-by-10-with-pause-subscriptions/), vendor). Recurly's cross-industry data puts the pause return rate at 75% ([Recurly](https://recurly.com/blog/why-pausing-a-subscription-can-be-a-powerful-retention-tactic/), vendor, mostly digital). Named-brand cases sit between 10 and 27% (OLIPOP 23.7%, Four Sigmatic 22%, Kettle & Fire 15%) ([Stay AI](https://stay.ai/blog/slashing-churn-with-retentionengine/); [Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/best-cancellation-flows-shopify-subscription-brands), vendor case studies). Plan on 10 to 20% at launch and 20 to 30% after several months of testing. Treat vendor claims of 30 to 35% as ceilings from mature programs.

Offer ordering is where most brands get it wrong. Operator Eli Weiss describes typical flows as "basically Mad Libs" that show the same 20% off regardless of reason, and notes that "if the customer has too much product, 20% off is just a cheaper way to make the problem worse" ([Eli Weiss](https://www.eliweisss.com/p/subscription-retention-churn-mitigation), practitioner). Practitioner guides agree that a discount "should come after all four options, if it appears at all" because default discounts train customers to cancel for price cuts ([Subscription Index](https://www.subscriptionindex.com/guides/cancellation-flow), practitioner). Ordergroove's data has skip access associated with subscribers lasting 135% longer and swap 71% longer ([Ordergroove](https://www.ordergroove.com/blog/from-cancel-to-keep-best-practices-that-retain-subscribers/), vendor, correlational). Part of that is selection (engaged people skip instead of cancelling), so the actionable lever is prompting the skip before the charge, not just having the button.

For Maison Croyez the flow writes itself from the cancel reasons and the brand rules.

- "I have too much." Offer: skip the next bottle, move the date, or change to 45 or 60 days. Mention the 50ml when it launches.
- "I want a change." Offer: swap to another intention, with imagery, in one click. Show which scents she has not tried.
- "It stopped working" or "I do not notice it anymore." Offer: the nose adaptation explanation, a schedule suggestion, and a swap. Route device faults straight to support with the warranty.
- "Too expensive." Offer: pause (self-resuming after four to eight weeks) or a longer cadence. If a monetary offer is ever used, make it a free 50ml on the next box rather than a percentage. A deeper percentage conflicts with "never compete on price" and, per the evidence, trains the behaviour you do not want.
- Every branch ends with a visible "Cancel anyway" that works immediately.

Keep it to one screen. Scentbird's multi-step flow (survey, 50% off, free fragrance, pause, with "Continue Canceling" in small type) coexists with "over 1.8K calls and complaints" ([FashionBeans](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/scentbird-reviews/), independent review). Minnesota caps save offers, and the 2026 FTC cases target flows that require reasons or gate cancellation behind pitches ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-sues-stop-sprawling-enterprise-operating-unlawful-subscription-schemes), regulator). So the reason question must be skippable, and pause is presented once as a declinable option.

Add a founder moment. Loop reports founder-led video in cancel flows reduces cancellations, and visual content lifts save rates "by 5 to 10 percentage points" ([Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/dtc-founders-reduce-subscription-cancellations); [Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/shopify-subscription-cancellation-flow-save-churning-subscribers), vendor). A 20-second clip of Patricia, calm and unhurried, saying she would rather send less than have a bottle sit unused, fits the voice exactly.

## Failed cards quietly take a quarter of churn unless caught

Involuntary churn (a card that failed and the customer never knew) is typically 20 to 40% of total churn for DTC brands on Shopify ([Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/why-subscribers-cancel-save-offers-that-work), vendor). Recharge's health and wellness split implies about 29% (1.2 of 4.2 points), Recurly's about 26% ([Eightx citing Recharge](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark); [Recurly](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/), vendor). Median dunning programs recover about half of failed charges; best-in-class recover 70 to 85% ([Recurly](https://recurly.com/resources/report/state-of-subscriptions/), vendor). Churn Buster reports a median 50% recovery for Shopify merchants and 60 to 70% for top performers ([Churn Buster](https://churnbuster.io/articles/shopify-dunning), vendor).

At 6% blended churn, 1.5 to 2 points are likely involuntary. Halving that is worth close to a full point of monthly churn, and it is the cheapest retention gain available. A 30-day cycle means twelve charge attempts a year per subscriber, so card expiry exposure is high.

The launch stack needs no specialist tool.

- Shopify Payments' network account updater refreshes expired cards automatically; Loop estimates this removes roughly 40% of failures silently ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/subscriptions/considerations); [Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/shopify-subscription-app-failed-payment-recovery), vendor). Confirm it is on.
- Set the app's retries to three or four attempts spaced two to three days apart, with "pause" (not "cancel") as the action after the last failure.
- Build a Klaviyo "Charge Failed" flow: email day 0, SMS day 1 with a passwordless update-card link, email day 3, final email day 6, suppressed on "Charge Paid." Recharge, Skio and Loop all send the event and support passwordless links ([Recharge Help](https://support.getrecharge.com/hc/en-us/articles/9828306941207-Using-Recharge-subscription-event-metrics-as-triggers-for-Klaviyo-email-and-SMS-flows); [Klaviyo Marketplace: Loop](https://marketplace.klaviyo.com/en-us/apps/01HVRHQ1E9960PPZK6BPV8W5X1/), vendor documentation).
- Add pre-dunning: a reminder 30, 14 and 7 days before a stored card expires. One blog claims 60 to 70% of customers update before the renewal attempt when reminded ([Revenudge](https://revenudge.com/blog/what-is-pre-dunning), aggregator, no primary dataset).
- Targets: 95%+ first-attempt success, 50 to 70% recovery, involuntary churn under 2% a month ([Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/why-subscription-payments-fail-on-shopify-and-how-to-fix-every-type), vendor).

Add Churn Buster later, once failed-payment volume justifies its fee. Scentbird's Recurly case study notes it cut failed transactions by optimising gateway approvals and adding Apple Pay ([Recurly](https://recurly.com/blog/scentbird-recurly-customer-story/), vendor); offering Shop Pay and Apple Pay at subscription checkout is the Shopify equivalent.

Shopify's native Subscriptions app is free but its Klaviyo integration "does not natively watch subscription billing events," so none of the lifecycle flows above can be built on it ([COREPPC](https://coreppc.com/shopify/shopify-klaviyo-tracking-integration/), agency). A third-party app is required. The market consolidated in April 2026 when Recharge bought Skio for $105 million; both continue to operate ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/y-combinator-alum-skio-sells-for-105m-cash-only-raised-8m-founder-says/), independent press). Prices below are from third-party and competitor blogs via excerpts and must be re-verified before contracting.

- Loop Starter: $99 a month plus 1.0% per transaction, no per-order fee, month to month, free to 50 subscriptions. Reason-branched cancel flows with page-level analytics, Klaviyo events, passwordless quick-action links, gifting. Shopify App Store 4.9 from 620 reviews ([Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/best-shopify-subscription-apps), vendor; [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/loop-subscriptions)). Not yet a Klaviyo Customer Hub subscription provider.

- Appstle: $0 to $100 a month, 0% fees, 5.0 rating from 8,300+ reviews. Budget fallback with swaps and Klaviyo events, less polished portal ([Easy Subscription](https://easysubscription.io/blog/best-shopify-subscription-apps/), vendor roundup).

Recommendation: Loop Starter for lowest all-in cost and the strongest cancel-flow analytics on the base tier, or Recharge Standard if you value prebuilt Klaviyo templates and Customer Hub more than the per-order fee. Either works. Do not spend a month deciding; the flows matter more than the app.

Klaviyo flows to build on the app's events. These map directly onto the five flows the bible requires that do not exist today (subscription welcome, win-back, replenishment reminder, VIP loyalty, post-purchase education).

1. Subscription Started: onboarding. Day 0 welcome and what happens next. Day 2 placement, mode and timer guide with expected bottle life per setting. Day 7 the story of her scent and its intention. Day 20 to 25 usage check: "Still have more than half? Move your next bottle back two weeks," with one-tap skip, push and swap links. This is the bible's missing Day 1/3/7/14/30 series and its day-25 replenishment reminder, merged.
2. Order Upcoming (three to five days before charge): email plus SMS to consented profiles. Ship date, amount, scent, one-tap skip, swap, move. Recharge defaults to three days; Skio recommends three to five ([Recharge Help](https://support.getrecharge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006356293-Notifications); [Skio Help](https://help.skio.com/docs/subscription-best-practices), vendor). Klaviyo flow SMS clicks about 10% against 5.6% for flow email, so SMS carries the time-sensitive message ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/sms-marketing/benchmarks), vendor). Include an annual variant with full terms and cancellation instructions for California and Minnesota compliance.
3. Charge Failed: the dunning sequence above.
4. Subscription Skipped: one calm email offering a cadence change. Two consecutive skips: cadence-change offer plus a note from Patricia. Never a discount.
5. Subscription Swapped: confirmation plus tips for the new scent and its intention. Reinforces that variety lives inside the brand.
6. Subscription Cancelled: reason-branched exit email, then win-back at days 7, 30, 60 and 90. Day 7 catches accidental and payment cancels. A four-touch reason-segmented sequence reactivates roughly 10 to 18% against about 6% for a single email ([Subscription Index](https://www.subscriptionindex.com/guides/win-back-campaigns); [Mailmend](https://mailmend.io/blogs/win-back-campaign-statistics), practitioner and aggregator; the Klaviyo primary source was not located). Former subscribers now make up about one in four new sign-ups industry-wide ([Recurly](https://recurly.com/content/state-of-subscriptions-report/), vendor). The win-back hook for "too much product" cancellers is timing (approach at 60 to 90 days when stock is plausibly gone); for "wanted change" cancellers it is a new or seasonal scent, not a discount.
7. Milestones (order count 3, 6, 12): surprise-and-delight gift notifications and the bible's VIP every-six-months flow. Recharge's Surprise & Delight adds a free one-time product to an upcoming order and can notify through Klaviyo ([Recharge Help](https://support.getrecharge.com/hc/en-us/articles/13753682375063-Building-a-Surprise-and-Delight-Subscription-Experience), vendor).

Turn off the app's native emails for any event Klaviyo handles, to avoid duplicates. Keep the app's transactional fallback for legally required notices.

Two Klaviyo flows that already exist should be checked against the new program. "MC Reorder Month 4-6" was built for one-time buyers and should exclude active subscribers. "MC Post-Purchase Upsell Free Diffuser per Scent" (draft) conflicts with the "no free diffuser" rule and should stay unpublished.

## Non-price benefits stretch the 25% further than a deeper discount would

The 25% subscriber discount is locked and this report works within it. Three facts frame it. It is the deepest no-commitment refill discount among direct competitors; Pura's 20% is the benchmark and Aera runs 10% ([Pura](https://pura.com/pages/subscribe-and-save); [Aera](https://www.aeraforhome.com/pages/subscriptions), vendor pages). Luxury refill programs outside subscriptions cluster around a 20 to 22% saving (Le Labo refills "approximately 20% off retail," Diptyque's new refill $70 against a $90 candle) ([Le Labo](https://www.lelabofragrances.com/le-labo-refill-program.html); [WWD](https://wwd.com/beauty-industry-news/beauty-features/exclusive-diptyques-iconic-candle-first-soft-lift-1238892302/), vendor and trade press). And no vendor publishes controlled data linking discount depth to retention; the practitioner consensus is that deeper discounts lift take rate but attract deal-seekers who churn faster, and that "a 15% discount that retains customers for 8 months beats a 25% discount with 4-month retention" ([Intelligems](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/blog/how-should-i-price-subscription-plans), vendor guidance). Segmenting by first-order discount depth "explains more retention variance than anything happening inside the ESP" ([Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/ecommerce-customer-retention-90-day-guide/), agency).

At the live price of $49.95, 25% off is $37.46 a month, with a refill cost of $10. That margin funds a lot of non-price retention. One extra retained month pays for roughly two free 100ml bottles including postage. So the strategy is to hold the discount where it is and layer benefits that cost little, that competitors already use, and that a 30+ woman reads more easily than points math. Each item below is a proposed addition to the locked offer stack and needs your sign-off.

- Subscriber warranty extension (proposed addition). The one-year warranty is locked; extending it to "as long as you are subscribed" is the Pura and Scentiment pattern. It costs nothing until a failure, and it turns the category's biggest churn trigger into a reason to stay.
- Milestone gifts at orders 3, 6 and 12 (proposed addition). Order 3: a free 50ml of a scent she has not tried (doubles as a swap prompt and adaptation reset). Order 6: a free 100ml. Order 12: an anniversary gift and early access to any seasonal scent. Scentiment gifts on the 3rd, 6th and 9th orders; Skio recommends rewarding orders 3, 6 and 12 "to shift the frame from spend to tenure" ([Scentiment](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/subscription); [Skio](https://skio.com/blog/why-70-of-supplement-subscribers-churn-after-order-2), vendor). Cost at COGS: about $5, $10 and $10 plus postage.
- Price lock (proposed addition). "The price you pay on your first refill is the price you pay forever" is Scentiment's line ([Scentiment](https://www.scentiment.com/pages/faqs), vendor). It directly answers the 31% who cancel over price without cutting price, and it protects subscribers from the $39.95 to $49.95 move the store already made.
- Prepaid 6 or 12 bottle plan (proposed addition). Annual prepay subscribers retain 28% at month 12 against 11% for monthly in Ordergroove's data ([Eightx citing Ordergroove](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-subscription-churn-by-billing-period-2026), vendor via aggregator; underlying dataset not inspected). The mechanism is simple: it removes eleven monthly decision points and eleven failed-payment opportunities. Price it at the same 25% per bottle, ship on her cadence, honour swap, and reward with fragrance (the seventh bottle free, or a 50ml discovery of each other scent) rather than a deeper percentage. Pause billing automatically on any open warranty ticket; Aroma360's record shows what happens when a commitment outlives a working device.
- Referral in fragrance credit (proposed addition). Pura pays $20 give, $20 get; Scentbird scales monthly referral credits with tenure ([Pura Rewards](https://pura.com/pages/rewards); [Scentbird](https://www.scentbird.com/blog/scentbird-referral-feature/), vendor). Pay the reward as a 50ml or refill credit, never cash off the diffuser. Median referred-visitor purchase rate is 3 to 5%, top quartile 8%+ ([ReferralCandy](https://www.referralcandy.com/blog/referral-program-benchmarks-whats-a-good-conversion-rate-in-2025/), vendor dataset of 3,200 stores).
- Second device as fragrance value (fits the stack). Multi-device households are where Pura's "$50 to 80 a month in vials" comes from ([WeTried.it](https://wetried.it/is-pura-worth-it/), independent review). No data was found on whether they churn less. Sell device two to active subscribers with a free 50ml or an upgraded two-bottle box, never with a hardware discount. One 100ml fits every device, which Aera's fragmented capsule sizes cannot match.

A points program is lower priority. With seven scents and one device the redemption catalogue is thin until the 50ml exists. Luxury programs that work reward with access and recognition rather than discounts ([BLOY](https://bloy.io/blog/luxury-loyalty-programs-how-premium-brands-build-loyalty-without-discounts/), vendor). If a loyalty app is added later, Smile is the Shopify-native default with confirmed Recharge integration ([Smile](https://smile.io/integrations/recharge), vendor).

Finally, the refill box. It is the only recurring physical touchpoint. Atlas Coffee Club pairs every shipment with a preview email, a guide and a post-tasting survey "to turn a monthly coffee subscription into a habit" ([Email Marketers Newsletter](https://emailmarketersnewsletter.com/p/atlas-coffee-club-brewed-22m-subscription-klaviyo-attribution-tips), practitioner). The equivalent here is a card per bottle with the intention, a timer and mode suggestion ("3 hours, medium, for bedtime"), and a QR code to the swap and cadence page. Scan rate by cohort becomes a pre-churn signal that no competitor without an app has. Diptyque's 2026 move to refillable candles, with refills "already a big share of the brand's fragrance business," is the premium precedent for making the refill feel like the luxury object rather than the cheaper substitute ([WWD](https://wwd.com/beauty-industry-news/beauty-features/exclusive-diptyques-iconic-candle-first-soft-lift-1238892302/), trade press).

## What this changes

The research reframes the bible's open question ("what is the right retention play at Month 6, 12 and 24?") as the wrong first question. By month six, the cohort that will reach month twelve is already mostly decided. The plays that move the twelve-month number are cadence matching, the pre-charge reminder, the day-20 usage check, dunning, and a save screen built on skip and swap. All of them act before bottle three. The tenure plays below are worth doing, but they are polish on a curve whose shape is set in the first 90 days.

The second shift is competitive. The category's growth leaders bought attach rate with free devices and clawbacks, and their review pages are the bill. Maison Croyez charges full price for the device, so it can be the brand that is easy to leave, and the evidence says being easy to leave is how you get people to stay.

## (b) Retention plays by tenure

Each play is a proposed addition to the locked stack unless noted. The evidence for tenure plays beyond month six is thin; these are grounded in competitor practice and operator guidance rather than measured outcomes, and the month-12 and month-24 plays should be treated as hypotheses to test.

Month 3 (order 3, the cliff).

- Free 50ml of a scent she has not tried, in the third box, with a card explaining why a second scent helps (nose adaptation). Cost about $5 plus postage.
- Cadence check message: "Three bottles in. Is the timing right?" with one-tap options.
- Turn on the subscriber warranty extension and tell her about it in this message. It is the moment device confidence matters most.

Month 6 (Recharge's 45% survival point).

- Free 100ml in the sixth box, chosen from the scents she has not subscribed to. Cost about $10 plus postage.
- The bible's VIP loyalty flow, every six months, fires here: a personal note from Patricia, and first access to any seasonal or limited scent.
- Invite to the prepaid 6 or 12 bottle plan with a fragrance reward. This is the natural moment: she has proved the routine and the plan removes the next six to twelve decision points.
- Second-device offer framed as fragrance value (free 50ml with device two), for households that have mentioned more than one room.

Month 12 (Recharge's 33% survival point, and California's annual reminder).

- The legally required annual terms reminder becomes an anniversary letter: a recap of her year (scents run, intentions chosen), full terms and how to cancel in plain language, and a gift (an anniversary 100ml or a keepsake tied to her signature scent).
- Price lock confirmation in writing: her refill price will not rise.
- Referral credits scaled by tenure, paid in fragrance.
- Ask for a review and a photo of her setup; routines that "can be shared or displayed socially" retain better in qualitative research.

Month 24 (no benchmark exists; evidence-light).

- Recognition rather than reward: a named status ("founding circle" or equivalent in the brand's voice), early input on new scents, an invitation to name or vote on a seasonal intention.
- A keepsake object connected to the ritual (an engraved or refillable vessel) to deepen the endowment effect without pressure.
- A no-strings check-in from Patricia asking what would make the ritual better. At 24 months the customer is the research.

## (c) Metrics from day one, with target ranges

All benchmarks below came from search excerpts; tags as before. Targets are the report's synthesis, set to beat the platform average because the program is being designed for retention from day one.

Leading indicators (weekly).

- Order-2 conversion (share of new subscribers who take the second charge). Benchmark: beauty 97.5%, health and wellness 92.9%, supplements 86.6% (Recharge, vendor). Target 90%+. Below 85% points to cadence or onboarding problems, not category.
- Order-3 conversion (share of starters reaching the third charge). Benchmark: supplements 57.6% (Recharge, vendor); best brands 50 to 60% past order three (Skio, vendor). Target 65%+.
- Time to first cancel attempt. Under 60 days signals broken onboarding (Skio, vendor). Target: median above 60 days.
- Skip, push and swap usage per cycle. 53% of Recharge subscribers adjusted an order in a year (vendor); supplements skip 6.9% of cycles (vendor). Track by cadence chosen. Two consecutive skips triggers the cadence flow. Rising skips on the 30-day plan means the default is wrong.
- Pre-charge reminder engagement. Klaviyo flow SMS click about 10%, flow email about 5.6% (vendor). Target at or above those.
- Refill card QR scan rate by cohort. No benchmark; establish a baseline and watch for decline ahead of churn.
- Cancel-flow save rate. Average about 15%, best 20%+ (Loop, vendor); pause alone 9.6% (Recharge, vendor). Target 10 to 20% at launch, 20 to 30% by month six. Also track 90-day survival of saved subscribers, which Loop calls the real metric.

Payment health (weekly).

- First-attempt payment success. Target 95%+ (Loop, vendor).
- Failed-payment recovery rate. Median about 50%, top 60 to 70% (Churn Buster, Recurly, vendor). Target 55%+ at launch, 65%+ by month six.
- Involuntary churn. Target under 2% a month (Loop, vendor), ideally under 1.5%.

Cohort survival (monthly, by signup month and by cadence).

- 90-day survival. Half of all cancellations happen inside 90 days (Everboost, independent). Target 70%+ of a cohort still active at day 90.
- 6-month survival. Benchmark 45% (Recharge, vendor via aggregator). Target 55%+.
- 12-month survival. Benchmark 33% platform-wide, 22% for small merchants (Recharge, vendor). Target 40%+; monthly-only programs at 11% (Ordergroove via Eightx) show the downside.
- Blended monthly churn. Consumer goods 6.5% (Recurly, vendor). Under 6% solid, under 5% top quartile (aggregator heuristics). Target under 5% by month six.

Value (monthly).

- Orders per subscriber per year. Benchmark 4.4 (Recharge, vendor). At $37.46 a bottle that is about $165 of first-year refill revenue per subscriber on average; a subscriber who reaches order 12 is worth about $450.
- Subscription take rate at checkout (share of scent purchases that choose the subscription). No public benchmark; the bible requires subscription to be the most prominent option, so track this from day one and set your own target after the first month.
- Win-back reactivation rate. 10 to 18% for a four-touch sequence, about 6% for one email (practitioner and aggregator). Target 10%+.
- Referred-visitor purchase rate. Median 3 to 5%, top quartile 8%+ (ReferralCandy, vendor dataset). Target 5%+.
- Cancel reasons, as a distribution. Loop's split (price 31%, overstock 16%, fatigue 15%, changed needs 15%, vendor) is the comparison. If overstock or fatigue exceed Loop's shares, the cadence and rotation design needs work. If price dominates, revisit the price lock and the non-price benefits before touching the discount.
